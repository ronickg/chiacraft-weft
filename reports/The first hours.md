# The first hours

First hours worker, 2026-11-05, for Ronald and Planner. It's the research behind the design in
`notes/FIRST_HOURS.md`.

Ronald (11-05): "the initial experience of playing the game ... you don't really have tools in
the beginning ... you sort of sharpen a rock", "you can't just punch down a tree", "wooden sticks
... lying around on the ground", "a bit of more survivally, but takes a bit longer ... you got to
feel like you earned it."

The full research, with every source (about 150 links):
- `reports/first-hours-research/prehistory.md`: how people really made their first tools, with
  experimental archaeology's numbers.
- `reports/first-hours-research/games.md`: how 14 survival games pace their opening, what players
  praise and complain about, and research on onboarding.

This file is the summary, and what it means for us.

## 1. What people really did first

| Step | What it is | How long, for real | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pick up stones | Rounded river cobbles as hammers, flint and chert nodules from chalk, gravel and river beds as cores. At Olduvai most stone came from within 2 km. | minutes of walking | [Schick & Toth](https://www.stoneageinstitute.org/pdfs/oldowan-ch1-schick-toth.pdf) |
| Strike a flake | A hammerstone on a core: hard-hammer percussion, 2.6 million years old. A plain sharp flake was the main knife. | seconds; novices get a usable flake every 2-3 blows | [Putt 2015](https://paleoanthro.net/media/meetings/files/Putt_2015.pdf) |
| A hand axe | A shaped core, held in the hand. | an expert 3-15 min; novices still poor after 90 hours of training | [Pargeter et al. 2019](https://emoryanthropology.org/2019/07/15/dr-justin-pargeter-and-colleagues-present-a-multidisciplinary-study-of-late-acheulean-handaxe-making-skill-acquisition-in-the-latest-edition-of-the-journal-of-human-evolution/) |
| Cord | Twisted plant fibre, bast, sinew. The oldest cord is 41,000-52,000 years old (Abri du Maras). | 20-50 min a metre by hand; retting bast takes weeks | [Hardy et al. 2020](https://www.pubmed.gov/32273518/) |
| Hafting | A head lashed or glued to a handle, about 500,000 years old (Kathu Pan). Lashing alone loosens when wet; birch tar holds. | a handle takes hours; a whole hafted axe a day or more | [Wilkins et al. 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23161998/) |
| Fire | Used for about a million years. The oldest made fire is flint and pyrite at Barnham, about 400,000 years ago. A bow drill gives an ember in under a minute with good wood; a hand drill averaged 3-4 minutes over 370 wood pairs, and often failed. | 1-4 min, with failures | [UCL 2025](https://www.ucl.ac.uk/news/2025/dec/british-discovery-shows-humans-made-fire-350000-earlier-thought), [Dayton 2023](https://udayton.edu/magazine/2023/12/lighting-fire.php) |
| Felling | A stone axe fells a 20 cm willow in 30-60 minutes. Steel is 3-6 times faster; bronze about as fast as steel. On saplings, stone took about 8 times the strokes of steel. | | [Draved 1952-54](https://core.tdar.org/document/420577/tree-felling-with-original-neolithic-flint-axes-in-draved-wood-report-on-the-experiments-in-1952-54), [Mathieu & Meyer 1997](https://core.tdar.org/document/413933/comparing-axe-heads-of-stone-bronze-and-steel-studies-in-experimental-archaeology) |
| Sticks | Deadfall, not cut wood. Wooden spears and digging sticks are among the oldest tools (Schöningen, redated in 2025 to about 200,000 years). | | [York 2025](https://www.york.ac.uk/news-and-events/news/2025/research/age-schoningen-spears) |
| Bone | Knapped bone tools are 1.5 million years old, and antler is the knapper's finer hammer. Then copper (about 7000 BCE), bronze (3300 BCE), iron (1200 BCE). | | prehistory.md, section 9 |

A modern primitive-skills practitioner (John Plant's Primitive Technology) took about 8 days to
peck and grind a basalt celt head, and a day and a morning for its handle
([his blog](https://primitivetechnology.wordpress.com/2017/11/24/new-area-starting-from-scratch/)).
Knapped flint is the fast way; ground stone is a later, slower tier.

**What it means for us:**
- Everything in the first hour is picked up, not cut: stones, sticks, grass, vines.
- The flake comes first, in seconds, and some strikes fail. It cuts; it doesn't chop.
- The real axe takes hours to days. Ours takes minutes, but it should feel like a step up, not a
  click.
- Plain stone (granite, sandstone) knaps badly; flint, chert and obsidian well.
- Felling ratios fit Felling's tiers: stone about 3-5 times slower than steel, copper and bronze
  closing most of the gap.

| TerraFirmaCraft | knapped stone knife or axe | about 5 | 5-15 / 1-3 | rocks underfoot; 5×5 knapping, about 8 clicks; the field guide | the long road to copper |
| No Tree Punching | flint knife, then flint axe | about 7 | 10-20 / 3-5 | short, believable, ends at flint | knapping by chance (28% a click), 25% fibre: feels like waiting |

Times are mostly our estimates from counting steps (no wiki times this moment); games.md says
which. Sources: TerraFirmaCraft's [source](https://github.com/TerraFirmaCraft/TerraFirmaCraft/tree/1.20.x)

**Things worth taking:**
- TerraFirmaCraft: logs drop nothing without an axe, but leaves give sticks by hand. Knapping is a
  5×5 grid of about 8 clicks. The fire is sticks and kindling on a block, lit by a firestarter at
  50% plus 10% a kindling.
- No Tree Punching: flint struck on stone gives shards; the flint knife cuts grass for fibre; the
  axe is shard, string and stick. Its hidden chances are what players dislike.
- Vintage Story: knapping can't spoil a head by removing a needed piece.
- Valheim: picking up the ingredients unlocks the recipe (our book's discovery already does this).
- Green Hell: lianas are rope.

## 3. What makes "earned" good, not tedious

From the games and the research (games.md, section 12):
- **One new idea per step** ([Hodent, GDC 2016](https://celiahodent.com/gamers-brain-ux-onboarding/):
  three things at once is the most a learner takes in).
- **The materials visible near the spawn.** Searching for the first ingredient is dead time.
- **Every click shows progress.** Hidden dice with nothing to see feel like waiting.
- **Failure that teaches, never failure that deletes.** The IKEA effect: labour makes people value
  a thing only when it ends in a finished thing ([IKEA effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IKEA_effect)).
- **The next step always hinted.** No hidden recipes on the main path.
- **Short repeated actions,** a few seconds each. Gathering is "the least engaging grind"
  ([AV Club](https://www.avclub.com/heres-why-survival-games-are-a-total-bore)).
- **Each step pays off at once:** the flake cuts grass, the cord makes the axe, the axe fells a tree.
- **About 5-7 steps and 5-15 minutes to the axe for a new player.** The long road belongs later.
- **Competence is what keeps people playing**
  ([Ryan, Rigby & Przybylski 2006](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/player-experience-of-needs-satisfaction-pens/)).

## 4. What we already have (dev, 11-05)

- **Felling:** bare hands chop the foot fruitlessly. The axe tiers: wood 40 logs, bone 64,
  stone and flint 96, copper 192 and up. A stopgap let bare hands keep the wood of trees up to 10
  logs; slice 1 takes it out.
- **The dead end slice 1 fixes:** planks, the table, every tool and the campfire needed logs,
  stone needed a pickaxe, and every tool recipe was 3 × 3.
- **Built already:**
  - plant fibre from grass (12.5%), sedge, cattail and lianas; string from 2 fibre;
  - tools from parts (head, haft, fitting), with grades and skills, assembled at the table;
  - the materials flint, chert, obsidian, plant cordage and sinew (rows, but no items yet);
  - leaves' 5% sticks;
  - stripping oak bark with an axe;
  - the first day at 10:00, half speed until dusk (about 13 real minutes).

## 5. The design that follows

It's in `notes/FIRST_HOURS.md`, with a picture. In short:
- rocks, flint and fallen branches on the ground, more near the spawn;
- strike a flake;
- cut grass for fibre and twist it into string;
- knap an axe head;
- lash it to a stick in the 2 × 2.

That's about 6 steps. A new player gets the axe in about 13 minutes, by dusk on the first day,
and someone who knows the way in about 3. The first fire comes next, from a fire bed lit with a
bow drill.

**Where we follow the lessons:**
- **The ground:** materials at hand near the spawn, guaranteed by a check.
- **Strikes:** each one sounds. The head's progress is kept in the nodule, and a spoiled head
  still leaves a flake (no work deleted). Slice 2's knapping screen makes the shaping visible.
- **Hints:** one per step, so the book isn't needed.
- **Recipes:** none hidden; the book shows the axe once you've held its parts.
- **The real ones:** flint over plain stone, deadfall, cord from grass.
