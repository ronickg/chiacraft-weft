# Recrafted v0.2.1: Polytone wiring, light, shadow and post modules (technique notes, own words)

Source: pack files read-only; everything below is a description of technique, not code. Constants are quoted only where they tell you the scale of the idea.

## 1. How Polytone turns a resource pack into a shader pack

Polytone (class families seen in the jar: `content/viewpoint/*`, `content/global_expressions/*`, `content/shaders/PostTargetsManager`, `ExpressionUniformBuffers`, `content/surfacemap/*`, `content/dimension/*`) adds five data-driven hooks to vanilla's resource system. Recrafted uses all five, and nothing else (no Iris, no OptiFine).

1. **Conditional overlays (pack.mcmeta).** 74 overlay directories, each with a `polytone_condition` that is either `require_config`, `require_mods` (the Sodium terrain overlay), or a free expression such as "AO || procedural water || reflections". Features are therefore folders: turn a config entry off and its shaders, post chains, blockstate tweaks and viewpoints vanish from the pack. Some overlays are shipped but hard-disabled (`false`): sun_path_rotation, menu_capture, procedural_ice, reflections_ultra. Overlays are the pack's "#ifdef" at asset level. `config_entries/*.json` declare the toggles, a UI section, order, a performance_impact label (shadows high, colored_lighting high, AO medium, bloom low, dynamic_lights low) and per-preset values (classic / balanced / complete), so quality presets are data.
2. **Extra post chains (`polytone/post_shaders` + `assets/recrafted/post_effect`).** A post_shader entry names a vanilla-format post-effect chain, gives it a numeric **priority** (execution order across ALL chains), an optional **activation_condition** (expression evaluated on the CPU, e.g. "dimension is overworld", "config shadows"), **expression_uniforms** (named std140 uniform blocks fed by CPU expressions every frame: rain, fog mode, sky/fog colour, held-light packed value, player item...), and `uses_viewpoints` (which extra camera renders it may sample). Priorities form the real frame graph: sky_lut 12, far_voxel 22, translucent_lighting 48, shade_composite 50, colored_lighting 56/57, translucent_shade 65, gtao 70, taa_mask 71, bloom_mask 77, transparency 80, fog 82, clouds 83, air_light 84, volumetric_light 85, anti_aliasing 86, motion_blur 92, light_bloom 93.
3. **Named persistent render targets (`post_targets`).** Declares targets that outlive one chain: scene_a/b/c (ping-pong full-screen colour handed from chain to chain), layer_i1/t1/t2 (item / translucent layers), taa_hist, shadow_hist, frame_state, and several **fixed-size "atlas" textures** (atlas_x 2306x1024, atlas_r 1154x1281, atlas_h 3104x6144, atlas_s...). These atlases are the pack's heap memory: each feature owns a rectangle ("atlas region" vertex shaders clip a full-screen pass to a sub-rectangle) and other chains read the data later in the frame. Weather field, colour-light grid, glass tint, far-shadow caster field, water caustic data, lightmap copy and a 3D light grid all live in them. 16x4 and 5x1 targets serve as little state registers (lightning_state, atlas_probe_latch).
4. **Extra viewpoints (`polytone/viewpoints`).** A viewpoint is a second (third, fourth) world render with its own expression-driven position and rotation, orthographic or perspective size, near/far, terrain layers (solid, cutout), whether to draw entities / block entities / particles, a resolution, a depth sampler name and colour sampler name that post chains can bind, a std140 uniform block carrying its view-projection matrix, an `activation_condition` and an `update_interval` (ticks; fractional ok). Three are shipped:
   - **near_shadow**: the sun's shadow map (see 3.3).
   - **heightmap**: straight down from y=320, ortho, 4096x4096, solid+cutout terrain only, no entities, refreshed every 200 ticks; its extent snaps to 256/512/1024 blocks by render distance and its position snaps to the texel grid (so the texels never swim). Samplers `InHeight` (depth = surface height) and `InHeightColor`. It is the base for far shadows, roofs/shelter, rain, snow, sand, reflection tracing, volumetric light and the coloured-light roof test. Its activation OR-chains every consumer plus a "warmup >= 1200 ticks" fallback so it renders once even if everything is off.
   - **colored_light_capture**: a 100-block ortho, 72-deep slab around the player, 768x768, every 4 ticks. It is not used to look at anything: it is a **voxelizer** (see 3.1).
   The camera for each is described by expressions that can read the real camera `c.x()`, the player `p.x()`, any environment attribute (`sun_angle`!), and global values, so a sun-aligned shadow camera is just maths in a JSON file.
5. **Global expressions (`polytone/global_expressions`).** Named scalar programs (a small expression language: ternaries, `min/max/clamp/floor/mod/sin/atan2`, locals, casts) evaluated on the CPU every `update_interval` ticks. Inputs: `g.time()`, `g.dayTime()`, `g.rain()`, `g.renderDistance()`, `g.dimensionType()`, `g.skyType()`, `g.seaLevel()`, `g.value('name')` (another or the same expression's value, last tick), `config('x')`, `c.*` camera, `c.environmentAttribute('minecraft:visual/...')` (the 26.x environment-attribute system: sky/fog/cloud colour, sun angle, fog start/end, sky_light_factor/color, ambient_light_color), `p.*` player (position, item names, eye block/sky light, onFire, temperature, downfall, swimming, canSeeSky, `p.at().up().north().block()`...). Output is one double, which becomes a uniform via `g.value(...)` in an `expression_uniforms` entry. Because `g.value('self')` reads the previous tick, **expressions are stateful**: integrators, latches, low-pass filters, counters.

Bonus hooks used: `surface_map.json` (Polytone keeps a chunk-cached "surface map" over render-distance: a height layer of the motion-blocking-no-leaves surface and a biome layer sampling fog_end, fog_start, fog_color, sky_color per column; exposed to shaders as textures; this is how fog, sky tint and climate vary per biome far away without a biome texture); `dimension_modifiers/end_sun.json` (overrides the End's sun_angle attribute so the End gets a light direction); `shader_modifiers/core/*.json` (feeds uniforms into vanilla core shaders: lightmap gets rain, item/entity get armor wetness and held light; also `PolyNoSun` = sky type to decide whether directional shading applies); and **Sodium** support through a second set of overridden terrain shaders in the sodium overlay (pack.mcmeta tells Sodium to ignore the stock terrain shaders). The pack also overrides vanilla `block.vsh/fsh` and `lightmap.fsh` directly.

**Data channels through RGBA8 framebuffers.** Everything shaders hand to each other is squeezed through 8-bit targets: the main colour target's **alpha channel is a G-buffer** (face id 0-5 encoded as 32+8*id; separate id ranges for leaves, foliage, entities, so later passes recover normals and material class without a normal buffer; the terrain shader writes it via `rc_terrainFaceIdAlpha`), multi-byte floats are split across channels (`frame_state` stores the previous camera matrices as float bits in alpha; the menu_capture pass stores 24-bit distance per pixel in RGB), and CPU values are packed into one double (e.g. air_state packs fog amount and daylight dimming into two 10-bit fields; lightmap_sky packs three 8-bit fields; held light packs a level and a tint id).

## 2. Exactly how wind and weather state are computed

**Wind (CPU side, global_expressions).**
- `wind_x = -1`, `wind_z = 0`: a constant prevailing direction (the shader include defaults to the same; a `RC_WIND_LIVE` build path can read them as uniforms and normalise).
- `wind_speed = (0.40 + 0.15*sin(time*0.01309) + 0.10*sin(time*0.004712)) * (1 + clamp(rain,0,1))`. `time` is `g.time()` in ticks; the two periods are about 480 and 1333 ticks (24 s and 67 s). So: mean 0.40, two slow incommensurate sine "breaths", then rain doubles it. This drives particles (leaves, pollen).
- `water_wind = (1 + 0.09375*sin(time*0.01309) + 0.0625*sin(time*0.004712)) * (1 + rain)`: same breathing at lower depth, rain doubling; it is a 1.0-centred scale for water.
- `water_phase`: accumulator `prev + (1 + storm*1.2) * 0.05 * run`, where `storm = clamp((water_wind - 1.2)/0.8, 0, 1)` and `run` is a 0/1 flag from `tick_gate`. So wave phase advances 0.05/tick, up to 2.2x faster in storms, and (because it integrates instead of using time*speed) changing wind never makes waves jump.
- `tick_gate`: a latch that outputs `2*floor(time) + flag`, where flag flips when the integer tick changed; it lets accumulators advance exactly once per game tick even if the expression is evaluated more often (render frames).

**Wind (GPU side, `wind.glsl`, used by animated grass/leaves).** Per block column: a gust field = value noise on an 8-block grid scrolled along the wind direction at 0.3 blocks/tick, with a spring-lag trick (instead of simulating springs, it samples the noise at a time offset equal to the mean spring lag, "collapse" mode) plus a 30% weight of a finer flutter noise. The local wind angle is the prevailing angle + gust*0.45 rad; local speed = base speed (same two-sine breathing as the CPU expression, recomputed from the time uniform so CPU and GPU agree) * (1 + 0.6*gust) * (1 + rain). A `shelter` helper mixes toward 0.15 where sky light is low so caves and under-canopy are calm. A ripple function (3 octaves, deep-water dispersion w ~ k^1.5, each octave turned 0.75 rad) turns wind into water-surface slope.

**Weather state.** There is no weather simulation in the pack. Everything hangs off vanilla's smoothed rain level `g.rain()` (0..1; 1 is rain and also thunder is read as a higher level, see below) and a few accumulators:
- `puddle_wet`: `prev + fall*0.0060 - (1-fall)*dryRate`, clamped 0..1, with `fall = clamp(rain*2,0,1)` and `dryRate = 0.00019 + 0.00037*sun`, `sun = clamp(min(t, 12000-t)/1500, 0, 1)` (t = day time). Wets in about 170 ticks of rain, dries 3-5x slower, faster at midday (and not at night). `puddle_active` is a 1600-tick countdown latch keeping the heavy puddle systems powered while wet.
- `armor_wet_*` per armour piece: relaxes toward targets (rain 0.9 only if the player can see sky and the climate is warm enough; submersion from a computed waterline; melted snow 0.6) at different rates (up 0.10, rain 0.006), dries by 0.001-0.003 (day) or 0.012 (Nether), and probes neighbouring blocks (lava, sponge, fire tags) to dry. Snow versions use temperature minus altitude lapse (0.00125 per block above y=80) vs a 0.15 freeze threshold.
- Lightmap weather leg: rain levels <= 0.5 fade sky light term from 1.0 to 0.55; 0.5..1 down to 0.3 (storm), `clamp(rain*2)`.
- Fog: `air_state` shortens fog end toward 96 blocks by `min(rain*2,1)*256`; also packs a day dimming factor from `dayTime` piecewise ramps (dusk 11867..13670, dawn 22330..133 over 1803 ticks).
- **Weather field** (priority 9 chain `weather_field`, runs when raining or still wet or during warmup): a 32x4 column pass reading the surface-map biome climate attribute, then writes into `atlas_r` a 320x320-texel field (4 blocks/texel, 1280 blocks across, snapped to 4 blocks so it doesn't shimmer). Each texel stores rain weight, a per-column **snow line** (always snow if temperature < 0.15, otherwise sea level + 17 + (temp - 0.15)*800 blocks, i.e. snow only above that Y, with a 40-block soft band) and climate. Rain/snow particles and the ground cover then ask "is it raining or snowing at this x,z,y?" instead of vanilla's one global flag; there is also a near-camera 32-cell, 60-block exact cell table.

## 3. Module-by-module

### 3.1 colored_lighting (high cost)
- **Sees:** block light that takes the colour of its source (soul fire blue, redstone red, crying obsidian purple, froglights, candles by dye colour, etc.) with a soft falloff, tinted light through glass; interacts with the lightmap; works at distance via a far-voxel fallback.
- **Technique:** a real voxel world representation built with vertex shaders. (a) **Marker geometry**: `blockstates` for ~180 emitters and shapes (torches, lanterns, walls, slabs-like shapes, beds, plants, glass, leaves) get an extra multipart model containing a sub-pixel-thin quad whose exact *position inside the block* (fractions of 1/128 block, so a coarse lattice of ~2048 steps) encodes a code: emitter id (4..253), glass tint id (256..319), leaf, shape mask (400+), plant species/orientation (3000+). Invisible in normal view. (b) The **colored_light_capture viewpoint** renders that world orthographically; the modified terrain vertex shader (and a Sodium variant) recognises the capture projection by its matrix signature, drops non-marker geometry, and **moves each marker vertex to the atlas texel of its voxel**: grid 96x64x96 cells stored as 8x8 vertical tiles of 96x96 in a 768² texture (cell → texel by y%8, y/8). The depth value written is a payload (class + id + light level), so the depth buffer *is* the voxel grid. This is vertex-shader voxelisation with no compute. (c) Post chain `colored_lighting` (20 passes, every 4 ticks): read columns (first open-sky cell per column, combining the voxel depth and the heightmap roof test), decode payloads into a cell struct, diff against the previous grid tile-by-tile and skip the fill when nothing changed (`diff` + `diff_any` gating via the `_gate` vertex shaders that collapse the triangle), then **15 flood-fill iterations** ping-ponging two buffers, each cell keeping the two strongest light sources (level + source id) so two colours can blend; light drops by 1 per step; faces closed by partial-block shapes (edge bits) block propagation; sky light is flooded the same way. A publish pass writes the final grid plus a lightmap copy and a brick "any light here?" 3D occupancy (`RC_CL_BRICK_SKIP`) into atlas_x. (d) The **composite** reconstructs world position and normal from depth + alpha face id, samples the grid trilinearly-ish with a half-cell drift correction for camera movement, converts light level + colour to an RGB gain by looking up the real lightmap at (level, sky), applies it to the finished frame and re-fogs it. Fallback `far_voxel` columns cover beyond 48 blocks using the heightmap.
- **Drivers:** block ids (emitter table), time (sky colour), rain, held item, camera movement; `update_interval` 4.
- **Worth copying:** (1) in your own engine you can simply keep a 3D light texture on CPU and upload it, so you need the fill, not the voxelisation. (2) the **two-strongest-sources-per-cell** representation is cheap colour mixing that avoids RGB x 15 channel BFS. (3) tile diff gating. (4) lighting as a *post gain* (new light / old light) so existing textures keep their shading.

### 3.2 dynamic_lights (cheap)
- **Sees:** held torch/lantern/glowstone/lava bucket/etc. lights nearby blocks and entities, with a glint on shiny surfaces; fades smoothly when you swap items.
- **Technique:** CPU: `held_light` expression looks up the main/off-hand item name via `endsWith` against a ~25-item table (torch 14, lantern 15, soul 10, redstone torch 7, lava bucket 15...) and low-passes toward it with factor 0.7254 per tick. `held_light_tint` fades a colour id in over time (0.7254 again) but snaps to 0 when the item changes. Shader: a point light at an approximate hand position (first person: right 0.40, up -0.35, forward 0.55 in view space; third person: from feet + hip offset, side by hand) with Manhattan-distance falloff (level minus L1 distance), applied as extra gain on the vanilla lightmap value in the vertex shader (blocks, entities, items), and in the post composite for the coloured-grid version; glint = power of the reflection toward the lamp.
- **Worth copying:** free dynamic light by feeding one light position+level+colour into the lightmap lookup; smooth fade by exponential approach.

### 3.3 shadow_map (high cost)
- **Sees:** sharp, soft-edged sun/moon shadows on terrain, blocks, entities, plants, translucent glass tinted, leaves letting some light through, foliage contact shadows, and the shadow also darkens ground light so torches matter at night.
- **Technique:** viewpoint `near_shadow`: ortho 66 blocks wide, near/far +-148, 1056x1056, solid+cutout terrain plus entities, block entities and particles, refreshed every 0.6 ticks (i.e. each frame at normal TPS). The camera expression derives the sun direction from the `sun_angle` attribute (rounded to 0.01 degree steps to avoid jitter), clamps the light to a minimum 10 degree elevation (so the shadow never goes horizontal at dusk), builds the camera yaw/pitch from it, and **snaps the camera origin to a 1/16-block grid along the light's right/up axes and to 16x8x16 world steps** (texel locking, no shimmer when walking). Center = player + light*108. Receiver: `shadow_mask` pass (full resolution but cheap early-outs) reconstructs world position, reads the face normal from the G-buffer alpha, offsets by a normal-aware bias (0.25 block), then samples the shadow map with **12-tap Poisson PCF** (2-texel radius, per-pixel rotated by blue-noise/IGN; block-edge aware branches so block faces get hard, aligned edges), receiver-plane depth bias, a one-sided-face terminator, and **transmission** for leaves (exponential-ish through-leaf attenuation D0 1.5 LAMBDA 1.5), foliage (0.25/0.5) and entities (0.25/0.3) instead of binary occlusion. Beyond the map it blends to the far-shadow field (3.4) over 8 blocks and adds **screen-space/voxel contact shadows** (rays up to 5 blocks, 8 steps, 4 supersamples, thin-plant windows) out to 96-192 blocks. Output is stored in a half-res-ish `shadowmap` target and run through a **temporal filter** (history + state registers) to hide noise, then `shade_composite` multiplies it into the scene: shadowed amount mixes toward the ambient/sky level and reacts to rain, fog, cloud shadows (a cloud coverage map projected to ground), held light and coloured light. Translucent surfaces get their own `translucent_shade` chain at priority 65.
- **Drivers:** time of day (sun angle), weather (cloud shadow and sky term), dimension sky type (`PolyShadowsOn` 1/2/3 for overworld / end / other), block tags (leaf/foliage ids), entities.
- **Worth copying:** the texel-snapped sun camera; Poisson PCF with per-pixel rotation; normal-offset bias via a cheap face-id channel; leaf transmission; the minimum sun elevation clamp.

### 3.4 far_shadow (moderate)
- **Sees:** long shadows of mountains, forests and buildings out to the whole render distance, matching the near map seamlessly.
- **Technique:** works on the 4096² top-down **heightmap**. Project the sun direction onto the map, and propagate "caster" info along that direction with a **doubling gather**: init pass picks, for every texel, the nearest upstream surface that shades it (height H, distance t) using the light slope c, then four step passes with strides 4, 16, 64, 256 texels each take the better of self, +n and +2n upstream (tap pair), i.e. a log-time horizon scan (like a prefix-min along a line). A `hm_erode` pass min-filters the heightmap (3x3 under a 5x5 max) to fatten thin objects so thin trees still cast. Pack caster as height + distance in an RG pair. Receivers compare their world Y against the caster line (H + t*slope) with soft 1-block edge and 0.15 bias; `FS_MATCH_MID_PCF` aligns the penumbra to the near map's.
- **Cost:** 7 passes on 1024x2048 targets, every frame the sun moves meaningfully; cheap.
- **Worth copying:** this is directly usable by us: one heightmap + log-step shadow-line propagation gives whole-world horizon shadows for almost nothing.

### 3.5 translucent_lighting (moderate)
- **Sees:** stained glass casting coloured shadows onto the ground and inside rooms, frames of panes casting thin lines, connected glass with no frames between neighbours.
- **Technique:** for each shadow-map texel, **DDA ray-march (<=256 steps) through the 96x64x96 voxel grid** from the shadow camera's near plane to the opaque depth, multiplying transmittance by the tint of every glass cell it crosses (panes are thin slabs at 7/16..9/16 with a 2/16 frame check that depends on whether neighbours are the same pane id when connected-glass is on). A `tl_bricks`→`tl_mask` pair first marks coarse bricks that contain any glass so empty areas early-out. Result stored in a 3104x6144 atlas region (`glass_light`), read by the shade composite so shadowed ground is tinted by glass colour.
- **Worth copying:** coloured glass shadows with a ray walk in the shadow map; the brick-occupancy skip.

### 3.6 directional_shading (cheap)
- **Sees:** block faces lit by the *actual* sun direction rather than vanilla's fixed up/down/side values; the shade rotates with the sun and flips to the moon at night, flattens in the End/Nether.
- **Technique:** the vanilla `block.fsh/vsh` and entity/item shaders divide the baked per-face shade (1.0 / 0.5 / 0.8 / 0.6) back out in the fragment shader, using face normal from `cross(dFdx,dFdy)` of the world position, then multiply by `0.5 + 0.5*(0.5 + (n.sun*0.5+0.5 - 0.5)*sunWeight)`, with sun weight a smoothstep on sun elevation and moon weight 0.4. A `PolyNoSun` uniform (1 for no-sky, 2 for End) disables or replaces with a fixed End light. A world-pos varying is passed from the vertex shader; flat `v_DirShade` marks real terrain vs. GUI (ortho check).
- **Worth copying:** zero cost, big visual gain: remove baked face shade and recompute from sun angle.

### 3.7 ambient_occlusion (moderate)
- **Sees:** contact darkening in corners, under stairs and trees, around entities; "classic" preset keeps it.
- **Technique:** two shaders: `gtao` (half resolution, 3 slices x 8 steps, radius 1.0 block, thickness 1/16 with distance-based thickening, visibility-bitmask style with rounded caps, blue-noise rotated) into `gtao_raw`; `gtao_composite` upsamples with **8 golden-angle depth-aware taps** (radius 12 px, depth-weight exp(-|dz|*4), IGN rotation), applies strength 3.0 with a floor 0.3, and protects bright pixels (luminance 0.75..1.05 fades AO out so lamps and sky-lit highlights don't darken), plus fog correction so AO fades in fog. Needs the `world_normal` chain (a reconstructed-from-alpha normal target) only in some modes.
- **Worth copying:** the highlight protection and half-res GTAO + depth-aware upsample.

### 3.8 light_bloom (cheap)
- **Sees:** soft glow around lamps, lava, sea lanterns, bright coloured light; held torch glow.
- **Technique:** `bloom_mask` first marks entity pixels (alpha id) to exclude them; `bloom_src` (half res) thresholds scene luminance with a smoothstep 0.35..0.75, a separate lower threshold for torch flames, strength 0.2, and uses the voxel light atlas so only *light sources* (not white walls) bloom; two **separable 49-tap Gaussian blurs** at 0.4 scale with two radii (core 0.01, wide 0.04 of screen) composited with weights 6 and 30 (a bright tight core plus a wide halo). Only active when colored lighting or shadows is on (needs the emitter knowledge).
- **Worth copying:** bloom driven by emitter knowledge, not luminance only; two-radius halo.

### 3.9 anti_aliasing (moderate to heavy)
- **Sees:** smooth edges and stable foliage; sharpened.
- **Technique:** chain of `taa_mask` (builds a per-pixel history weight using layers: item/entity/particle/translucent layers each get their own policy), `taa` (reprojection using last frame's camera matrices which the previous frame stashed in the `frame_state` texture; YCoCg neighbourhood variance clipping, alpha 0.08 strong / 0.5 weak depending on motion 1..12 px, hole detection by depth ratio, anti-crawl rules for slow sub-pixel drift, special weaker handling of vines and edges, history sharpen 0.25), `taa_sharpen`, then **SMAA** (edge, weight, blend with the area/search lookup textures) that fades in when TAA history is invalid or the camera is still. No sub-pixel jitter pass was found in the shaders I read; the chain depends on the history being valid and the SMAA fallback covering static edges. Cost: 5 passes full res.
- **Worth copying:** TAA that falls back to SMAA on invalid history; layer-aware history masks.

### 3.10 motion_blur (moderate)
- **Sees:** camera motion blur with a stable hand, no smearing across depth edges.
- **Technique:** `motion_blur_flow` builds a per-pixel velocity from depth + stored camera motion (the same `frame_state`), with 9-tap searches and clamp to 9% of screen; `tilemax` (1/16 res) and `neighbormax` produce the McGuire-style neighbourhood max velocity; the blur pass takes 6..48 taps (1.3 px spacing along the vector), soft depth comparison (10% relative, 0.5 abs), excludes the hand (depth 0.25..0.60), exposure-based shutter scaling by frame time (reference 1/3 s). Last pass saves camera state into the 1x1-ish register with float bits written into the alpha channel.
- **Worth copying:** the tile-max architecture; storing the previous camera matrices through a texture register.

### 3.11 recrafted_lightmap (cheap)
- **Sees:** nicer day/night/dusk sky light curve: a pow-4 sky brightness, a dusk lift that warms/raises light at sunset, stormy rain darkening (weather leg 1 -> 0.55 -> 0.3), soft highlight, gamma-ish "notGamma" brightness setting. Rebuilds the 16x16 vanilla lightmap in `lightmap.fsh`; a CPU expression `lightmap_sky` packs sky factor / ambient / sky colour so post chains can reproduce the vanilla lightmap exactly (haze gain = ours/vanilla).
- **Worth copying:** the dusk lift ramp and rain leg; treat the lightmap as the single tuning point.

### 3.12 world_normal (cheap, support)
- A chain that reconstructs normals (opaque and translucent) into a target using depth derivatives and the face-id alpha, so AO, reflections and water can use them. The opaque one is disabled (`activation_condition: 0`) because normals come from the alpha id; the translucent normal chain (priority 11) is on only for procedural water or reflections: it reads translucent depth, wake data, atlas_r/h, and rain/wave phase. Worth copying: encode a face id in spare alpha rather than a G-buffer.

### 3.13 sun_path_rotation (off by default)
Tilts the sun/moon path by 40 degrees around the x axis (the stars vertex shader and sky quads), so the sun traces an off-vertical arc and shadows never point exactly east-west. Shipped but gated by `false`; the directional-shading include already applies it to light direction if enabled. Cheap, one rotation matrix.

### 3.14 sodium_terrain (cheap)
Replacement `block_layer_opaque` vsh/fsh for Sodium so the same features (directional shading, face-id alpha write, translucent water depth tag, coloured-light voxel capture, dynamic light) work in Sodium's terrain pipeline; a smooth-light rule constant exists (0 = off). Condition: Sodium loaded.

### 3.15 armor_weathering (cheap per frame, heavy expression)
Armour gets wet (darker, glossier), snow-dusted, or sand-dusted per piece. Eight `armor_*` global expressions per piece integrate wetness/snow/sand from rain, submersion level (a waterline computed from fluid blocks around the player), temperature minus altitude lapse, standing/moving, neighbouring block probes, fire/lava/sponge drying; values go to entity/item core shaders as uniforms (`PolyArmorWet*`, `PolyArmorWear*`, `PolyArmorEffect`), and the shader blends a darkening/frost/sand mask by height up the model. Worth copying: per-piece integrators with different rise/fall rates.

### 3.16 menu_capture (disabled, debug)
A chain that writes camera intrinsics (inverse projection scales, forward vector) into the last row of the frame and 24-bit world distance per pixel into RGB (sky = max), apparently for external tooling/backdrop; gated by `false` and, in its own post_shader, by holding a stick. Not a player feature.

### 3.17 hide_blob_shadow
Just replaces the vanilla entity blob-shadow texture with an empty one when real shadows or AO are active. Trivial and necessary.

## 4. Cost picture and what to steal first
- Heavy: shadow_map (1056² render of the world again, plus full-res mask with 12 taps), colored_lighting (a full 100-block slab re-render every 4 ticks + 20 passes), TAA/SMAA, volumetric/air chains (not in scope).
- Moderate: far_shadow, translucent_lighting, GTAO, motion blur.
- Cheap: directional shading, dynamic light, lightmap, bloom, wind/weather expressions.
For chiacraft the best-value ideas, in order: (1) heightmap + log-step horizon shadows (far_shadow); (2) texel-snapped sun camera with min-elevation clamp, Poisson PCF, leaf transmission; (3) stateful per-tick accumulators for wetness, puddles, wave phase, wind breathing (two-sine wind, rain multiplier, rain-wet integrator with sun-scaled drying); (4) weather as a field with a temperature-derived snow line instead of a global flag; (5) directional shading recomputed from sun angle; (6) dynamic hand-light with exponential fade; (7) two-strongest-source coloured light cells and tile-diff gated propagation; (8) coloured glass shadows by DDA through the same voxel grid.
