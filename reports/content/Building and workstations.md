# Building and workstations: real houses, materials and crafts

Research for the content plan: what people really built in each biome's real place, how well each
material keeps heat in or out, how the materials are made, what workplace each craft needs, and
what furniture a real home had. Section F turns it into a proposed block, workstation and furniture
set. 2026-11-03.

**How sure is it.** Numbers with a source carry its link [n]. Numbers marked *est.* are building
physics common knowledge or my arithmetic. The heat numbers are solid where a study measured them
(the snow house [1], the snow walls [2], the rammed-earth houses [3]) and rough elsewhere: nobody
has logged a sod house or a tipi with a thermometer in a paper I could find. Craft times come from
experimental archaeology and living-history sites. They vary twofold or more by worker and weather.
Llanos and Pantanal houses are the thinnest: I found no study of the casa llanera or the casa
pantaneira, so those rows are general Latin American practice, marked *est.*

**One block is one metre.** Real walls are 0.1-0.9 m thick. The game should rate a wall by its
material class, not by physics on a 1 m slab, or a log wall would insulate like 6 real log walls.

## B. How well each material keeps heat in or out

### B1. Material numbers

`k` is conductivity (lower keeps heat better). `U` is heat loss per m² per °C through a wall of
the usual real thickness, including surface films (0.17 m²K/W) *est.* arithmetic. `Mass` is how
much the wall evens out day and night.

| Material | k W/m·K | Usual wall | U W/m²K | Mass | Src |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Softwood log | 0.12-0.14 (R-1.41/inch) | 20 cm | ~0.6 (more with leaky chinking) | medium | [6][7] |
| Hardwood | ~0.17-0.2 (R-0.71/inch) | - | - | medium | [6] |
| Sod / turf | ~0.5-1.0 *est.* (moist soil) | 60-90 cm | ~0.8-1.3 *est.* | high | [14] |
| Adobe | 0.52-0.57 | 40-60 cm | ~0.8-1.0 | high | [10] |
| Cob | 0.52-0.6 | 60 cm | ~0.85 | high | [8] |
| Rammed earth | ~0.75-1.0 *est.* | 50-55 cm | ~1.3-1.6 *est.* | high | [3] |
| Wattle and daub | ~0.6-0.8 *est.* | 10-15 cm | ~2.5-3 *est.* | low | [32] |
| Fired brick | 0.6-1.0 | 23 cm | ~2.0 | medium-high | [7][9] |
| Limestone | 1.1-1.7 | 50-60 cm | ~1.7-2.2 | high | [8] |
| Granite / basalt | 1.7-3.5 | 60 cm | ~2.2-3 | high | [7][8] |
| Packed snow (igloo wall) | 0.27 (cave 0.36, quinzee 0.56) | 20-30 cm | ~0.8-1.0; whole iglu measured 2.24 incl. leaks | low (melts above 0) | [1][2] |
| Fresh snow | ~0.03-0.1 | - | - | - | [2] |
| Reed thatch | 0.09 (long straw 0.07) | 30 cm | ~0.3 | none | [11] |
| Straw bale | 0.09 | 45 cm | ~0.2 | low | [7] |
| Wool felt | 0.04-0.07 | 3-5 cm (yurt) | ~1.0-1.3 *est.* | none | [9] |
| Hide (tipi) | ~0.15 *est.* | 3-5 mm | ~5-6 single; ~2.5-3 with liner *est.* | none | [20] |

What this means for the game:

- **Thatch and logs insulate; stone and brick don't.** A thick stone wall loses three to four times
  the heat of a log wall. Stone's job is mass and fire safety, not warmth.
- **Earth (sod, adobe, cob, rammed earth) is a middling insulator and a big heat store.** It holds
  the daily average, not a warm or a cool temperature.
- **A hide tent barely insulates.** It stops wind. The liner is what makes a tipi livable [20].

**Adobe at noon.** Mass pulls the inside toward the day's mean. Hermosillo in July runs about 26 to
40 °C *est.* (mean 33). A thick adobe room sits near 31-34 °C all day *est.*: 6-9 °C cooler than
outside at the 15:00 peak, but warmer than outside before dawn. The heat arrives inside about 5
hours late [3]. That is why desert people slept outside or on the roof in summer and shaded the
walls with ramadas.

### B3. Proposal for the game (heat)

My proposal, built from the numbers above. One fire heats a room by `heat / loss`. Each wall face
of a shelter adds loss by its class; the room's mass smooths the day.

| Thing | Effect (proposal) | Based on |
|---|---|---|
| Any roof overhead | Night: +2 °C (blocks the cold sky). Sun: -4 °C felt (shade) | *est.* |
| Wind shelter (3+ walls) | Removes wind chill | - |
| Empty closed room, no fire | +2 °C over outside (+5 on the ground in snow) | [1] |
| Body heat, per player or tame animal in a small room | +1 to +2 °C | [1] |
| Fire, in the open | +6 °C within 2 blocks, fading by 4 | *est.* |
| Fire, under a roof only (ramada, lean-to) | +8 °C near it | *est.* |
| Fire or lamp in a closed room | hide/felt walls +12; wattle, plank, stone, brick +16; log, sod, adobe, cob, snow +25; thatch roof instead of another roof +3 | [1] and B1 |
| Seal-oil lamp in a snow house | +20 to +40 (small room, snow walls) | [1] |
| Masonry stove (Russian stove, tile stove) | like a fire +25, keeps giving heat 12-24 h after the fire dies *est.* | [22][23] |
| Daily swing inside (share of outside swing) | earth, stone, adobe, sod: 0.15, lag 5 h; log, brick: 0.4; plank, wattle: 0.6; hide, felt, snow: 0.9 | [3] *est.* |
| Snow walls | Melt if inside goes above +5 for long *est.*; skins on the inside stop it | [1] |
| Wet thatch or sod | Insulates worse when soaked (thatch k rises with moisture) | [11] |

Winter evenings were craft time: in cold places most making of clothes and tools happened indoors
by the light of the lamp or fire. The game can reward this: crafting at a station inside a
comfortable house goes faster or gives better quality *est.*

**Comfort rule of thumb (proposal):** a house is "rested" when it has a heat source, a roof, a bed
or sleeping platform, a place to sit, and one of light, rug or hanging. Biome pieces (hammock,
qulliq, Russian stove) count as the same comfort type as their general piece, so every biome can
reach full rest with its own furniture.
