address 191.177.192.179, so a "public" path is a hairpin through that router. PC to Mac ping, 20

dev 7f247cfe (Upstream's path log), mobs off, creative, testnet11 only. Path line format:
`net: path to <id> relay|direct|mixed <LAN|public|VPN|loopback> <addr> (join | player in | portal arrival)`.

the first path of a portal arrival (second and later visits) and the connection then moved to
mixed LAN and mixed public within 2-6 s. No errors, no disconnects, no lag problems.

## What stood out

1. **The path is direct, not relay, on one LAN.** The Mac and PC joined over 192.168.1.x every time.
   Upstream explained the changes between LAN and public: iroh's biased RTT path selector ranks
   paths by round-trip time and switches when another path is at least 5 ms faster; on jittery
   Wi-Fi the router's hairpin address sometimes wins. Both paths go through the same router and
   Wi-Fi, so speed is the same. Pinning to LAN needs a custom PathSelector behind iroh's
   `unstable-custom-transports`, which Upstream does not recommend. Watch instead for a `relay`
   line that stays, or a flip every few seconds.
2. **Relay shows up first on portal arrivals** (second visit, and bob's trip to A), then the path
   moves to mixed LAN / mixed public in 2-6 s. The first visit connected direct at once.
3. **Return through the portal is easy to miss.** A guest arrives in a free cell BESIDE the portal
   (the first free cell east of it), and walking straight ahead along that column passes the
   portal without touching it. Stepping one block sideways into the portal works. For a player this
   is a puzzle, for a test it is a gotcha. (Portals mod: `arrival_spot()`.)
4. **DID check on arrival** can say "no chain peer reachable and the DID isn't cached" when the
   chain read has just started, and then lets the player in unverified. Worth Upstream's attention:
   whether a portal arrival should wait for the DID check or retry.
5. **Find Worlds took about 3 min** to show a freshly listed world (first two reads too early).
6. **PC ufw was on** and did not stop the Mac-to-PC or PC-to-Mac direct paths (the PC's outbound
   works, and the Mac has no firewall). A relay fallback would also count as relay-path proof.
