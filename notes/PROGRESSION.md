# Progression

The system behind items, resources, bosses, magic, taming, automation and exploration: what a
player does from the first flint to the stars, and how new things are added later without
breaking it. This is a first draft from Ronald's answers on 2026-11-06. Nothing here is built
yet. The early ages already exist in pieces: `notes/FIRST_HOURS.md`, `notes/CRAFTING.md` (the
material ladder) and `notes/ANIMALS.md`.

## The lore: the star-born ancients

Ages ago a civilisation came from the stars, built the ley network and fell. What it left is
what the player finds:
- meteorites, star steel and pallasite (already in the game);
- the ley lines, which still carry magic;
- ruins with their blueprints, relics and lore pages;
- the biome bosses, once the ancients' guardians, now wild;
- the dimensions, which were their worlds, reached through their gates.

Each age the player understands more of them. The future age is reaching the stars they came
from, and the final boss waits in the furthest dimension. Magitech fits this: their tech ran on
magic, so ours learns to as well.

Each age is a tier number. Hours are a first budget for the 100+ hour target.

| 2 | Iron | Forging, steel, star steel | Spells from found tomes; mana from ley nodes | Mounts, war beasts, breeding lines | 16 |
| 3 | Steam and industry | Boilers, belts, rail, the first machines | Ley taps feeding machines (magitech starts) | Tames on treadwheels and in pens with feeders | 18 |
| 4 | Electric and digital | Power grids, the storage network, autocrafting | Runic circuits, crystal batteries | Genetics machines, tames linked to the network | 20 |
| 5 | Future | The power suit, flight, drones | Star cores, rituals that bind tech | Cyber and star-bonded beasts, tech saddles | 18 |
| 6 | Star | Gates and ships to the ancients' worlds | The ancients' magic | Creatures from other dimensions | 16 |

Total about 106 hours. The ladder stays open at the top: a tier 7 can be added later.

## How an age is gated

Moving to the next age takes an age key: one thing from each of the four gates.
- **Materials and tools:** the next age's ore or material needs the last age's tools to mine or work, as the material ladder does today.
- **A biome boss:** at least one of the age's bosses gives a part the key needs. Any one of several will do, so a player can pick which biome to explore.
- **Knowledge:** a blueprint or lore page found in that age's ruins.
- **Infrastructure:** a working setup from the age before, such as a power grid before electric machines.

Inside an age, the three paths each have their own branch (tech, magic, beasts). An age's key
needs some of each, so none can be skipped, but a player leans on the one they like.

## The systems

### Skills and perks (like Skyrim)
Ronald, 11-06: "like Skyrim, where you have your smithing progression ... just allows for more
customizability". The model is Skyrim's:
- **Skills rise by doing.** Forging raises Smithing, shooting raises Archery, taming raises Taming. A skill's level sets which of its perks can be taken.
- **Skill-ups feed your level.** Each level gives a perk point to spend in any tree. Points are scarce, so a player specialises; a rare item resets them.
- **Yours only.** Skills, perks and age are always the player's own, even in a tribe.

**One web, like Path of Exile's passive tree** (Ronald, 11-06: "one thing in the center, and then
you sort of expands out in the different branches ... you can kind of be a jack of all trades, but
not really. But you can also then get very specific"):
- Every perk sits on one big web. You start in the centre and spend points on nodes next to ones you have, growing outward.
- Each group of skills below has its own region of the web, so going deep in one means not reaching the far end of another.
- **Small nodes** give small bonuses (+2% melee damage, faster taming).
- **Notables** are the key ones worth walking to (a tame shares your buffs, spells chain to a second target, forged items roll an extra property).
- **Keystones** at the edges change how you play, with a cost. For example: your tames fight twice as hard but you can't wear heavy armour; or spells cost health instead of mana.
- **Skill levels gate depth.** A region's deeper nodes need that skill's level, which rises by doing (Skyrim). So you can only go deep where you actually play, but your points decide which way.
- **Bridges between regions** reward hybrids (between Beasts and Magic: rune collars; between Crafting and Engineering: machine-forged gear). A jack of all trades is possible but thin; a specialist goes far.
- **Respec** with a rare item, a few nodes at a time.
- **Room to grow:** new ages and content add nodes at the web's rim or new branches off existing notables, so old builds keep working.

The regions, grouped:

| Group | Trees |
|---|---|
| Fighting | Melee (blades, axes, spears), Archery and throwing, Defence (shields, armour) |
| Magic | Destruction, Restoration, Conjuring and wards; each school's perks open its stronger spells |
| Crafting | Smithing and tinkering, Alchemy and cooking, Runecraft and enchanting, Building and farming |
| Beasts | Taming and riding (Ronald: "also taming too") |
| Proposed by Planner | Mining and prospecting, Engineering (machines and automation), Exploring and cartography, Trading, Survival (fishing, tracking, weather) |

**Built 11-05** (branch claude/perk-web-a4441b): the 16 skills rising by doing, priced in effort
minutes with tier against skill (`vitals::skills`); one web of 288 nodes, a region per skill from
one template, bridges, keystones, depth gated by skill, refunds with the Unlearning Stone

Crafting perks follow Smithing's shape: perks unlock working the next tier's materials, better
grades and rolls, repairs, salvage, and later merging and upgrading items (see "Gear that keeps
getting better").

### Gear that keeps getting better
Ronald, 11-06: "have a look into how Path of Exile 2 does things ... where you can effectively
keep on playing and you can always end up getting something better or merging something to get it
better". So gear never tops out:
- items roll properties inside their tier's band, as spells do;
- they can be improved, rerolled, merged or upgraded with materials found by exploring and fighting;
- the endgame keeps dropping better rolls.

How exactly waits on the research round (Path of Exile 2's crafting currencies and endgame maps,
among others).

### Biome bosses
- Every biome has its own: a giant creature native to it (a sand wyrm, a frost mammoth).
- It's summoned at an altar with the biome's offerings, so the player chooses when to fight.
- Each boss has a harder form for each later age, with better loot.
- Loot is random from the boss's table, with bad-luck help: every kill without a piece raises that piece's chance.
- After a win, the player can tame a young one, or the boss drops an egg.

### Magic
- **Runes and glyphs:** inscribed on tools, armour, blocks and machines. Patterns are found by exploring, and later runes become circuits.
- **Spells and mana:** mana comes from ley lines and crystals, and spells sit in a Mage skill tree, like Skyrim's.
- **Spells as gear:** a spell tome or focus rolls its quality like a crafted weapon (range, power, cost), so a better lightning can be found or made.
- **Rituals at altars:** bigger effects, such as summoning a boss, blessing a field, opening a gate, or binding a tame.
- **Enchanted gear:** wands, staffs and charms built from parts, like tools are.

### Ley lines
- Magic flows along ley lines, with nodes in every biome.
- Where you build matters: magic and magitech draw from the nearest line, and late automation taps them like a power grid.

### Exploring
- **Ruins of the ancients:** in every biome. They hold blueprints (the only way to some of the best gear), relics (unique items with special effects), lore pages that point to the next ruin, lair or gate, and rare life (seeds, eggs, creatures found only far away).
- **Expedition maps:** charts the player draws and shares, marking finds. The Explorer tree reveals ruins and lairs.

### Tames
- **Early:** work and farming (ploughing, hauling, gathering, milling) and power before machines (mills and treadwheels).
- **Middle:** mounts open far biomes and lairs before vehicles exist.
- **Breeding:** creature genetics pass on traits (strength, yield, speed, colour mutations), and later machines splice them.
- **Gear:** saddles and armour go up the ages, ending in tech saddles; magic bonds give powers.
- **Late:** in the future age, cyber and star-bonded beasts and creatures from other dimensions; tames link to the storage network (a pack beast as a mobile terminal, a flyer running deliveries).

### Automation
- It starts with steam (belts, the first machines) and grows into an ME-style storage network with autocrafting in the electric age.
- Magitech runs on it too: ley taps, runic circuits and crystal batteries.

### The transport ladder
Pack animals and carts, then rail and boats, then airships, drones, teleporters, and finally
ships and gates to other worlds.

### The power suit
- It's built from parts like our tools: a frame from tech, a core from magic (a star core or ley crystal), and plating from a boss.
- Each path adds modules: flight, a shield, a link to your tame.
- It starts in the future age and grows through the star age.

### Dimensions
- They're linked worlds through our portal system (`notes/PLAN.md` phase 3), each with its own biomes, boss and materials, and a tier it needs.
- Servers can host them, and new ones are added later as packages.

### Settlements and traders
NPC camps and towns that trade, give contracts and sell knowledge. They grow with the ages, from
a trading post to a city with a spaceport.

### Tribes
- **Shared:** knowledge (a shared tribe journal; making something still needs its materials and tier), boss and world deeds (a kill or an opened dimension counts for everyone present), and bases, tames and the storage network, by rank.
- **Own:** skill points, age, your pack and private chests.

### Death
- You drop everything at the spot and run back for it, as in Ark or Valheim.
- A world setting can soften it.

### Peoples (Ronald's answers, 11-06)

Ronald: "I really love the fantasy stuff, like the Lord of the Rings with elves and dwarves ...
some dwarven kingdom in like the mountains, or elves would be in a specific biome".
- **Who:** dwarves in the mountain ranges (kingdoms carved into the rock, master smiths and miners, deep halls and forges), elves in the old forests (the sequoia groves or the tropical canopy; keepers of ley magic, archers and beast-tamers), humans as the main folk in the open lands (the settlers and traders, growing with the ages), and more peoples later, each in its own biome (desert nomads, swamp folk, sea raiders, northern giants).
- **Lore:** heirs of the ancients. Each people inherited one part of the star-born ancients' legacy: dwarves their craft and star steel, elves their ley magic, humans their spread. Their old feuds and lost knowledge are what the player uncovers.
- **Dealing with them:** reputation per people (trading, helping and contracts raise it, and higher standing opens their smiths, spells, mounts and lands). They teach their crafts (dwarves smithing secrets and blueprints, elves magic and taming), which ties them into the skill web and the ages. They can turn hostile: guards and ambushes.
- **In the world:** living towns and kingdoms with NPCs going about their day, traders, smiths and quest givers. A big build.
- **Names:** each people gets its own naming language, as Tolkien's do (`reports/Naming and invented words.md`). The ancients speak Old Eskenai (Ronald: "Yes, use Eskenai"). Elves speak a soft daughter of it, and can live in several forests ("we can add more ones for them"). Dwarves have their own tongue, and wear English bynames with true names learned through reputation ("Yes, bynames + true names"). Guardians' names end in -kaun or -gom (Borkon, the Height-Keeper). The naming tool comes first ("Now"), so names never get made up freehand. Built: `tools/names/names.py` over `mods/base/lore/lexicon.toml`, with the sheet `notes/NAMES.md`.

### Endless gear: the research's proposal

Ronald, 11-06: yes to item level and found materials, merging, the star-chart endgame, and a death in a pocket world costing the run, not the gear. Not chosen: rebasing, boss essences, rerolling.

From `reports/Progression systems.md` (Path of Exile 2, Skyrim, Ark, Terraria, Vintage Story,
Valheim, GregTech and the automation packs, Thaumcraft and Botania). It builds on
`notes/CRAFTING.md`'s parts and ten grades, and keeps its rule of no rerolls to buy:
- **Item level:** tier × 10, plus a depth of 0-9 from where the material or drop came from (a ley vein, a boss form, a dimension's depth). It caps which properties can roll.
- **Marks:** properties (affixes) with item-level tiers, added only with found crafting materials, each material with one job: ley shards, star dust, pallasite tears and others, from ley nodes, ruins and meteorites.
- **Merging:** the ancients' fusing anvil pools two items' properties into one, with risk. A relic's potential (1-4) caps how much transfers.
- **A repeatable endgame:** star charts open pocket worlds through the gates at depths 1-16. Deeper charts drop deeper charts and higher item levels, and the Exploring and cartography region of the web plays Path of Exile's Atlas tree.
- **Boss forms:** one per age, and each higher form gives everything the lower ones do plus more. A boss's drop is a key or tool that opens the next material.
- **Power-creep controls:** each tier is about 1.6× the last. Grade plus marks add at most +60%, so a perfect tier-N item roughly equals a plain tier-N+1. Skill experience comes from an item's tier against your skill, not from how many you make. Enemies scale by place and age, never by your level. A crafting simulation in the sheet flags any source that's too generous.
- **Bad-luck help:** certain by a set kill count.
- **Death in a dimension:** costs the expedition, not your gear, softening "drop everything" there.

## How it stays balanced when things are added

Five rules, kept in code and checked by tests, so a new item, boss or branch can't quietly
break the path.

1. **Every thing has a tier and a path.** Each item, block, recipe, boss, blueprint, spell and tame role declares its age (tier number) and path (tech, magic, beasts, shared). It's data in a shared crate, like `vitals` holds survival's numbers, never loose in a mod.
2. **Stats come from curves, not hand-tuning.** Each tier has a power budget: damage, armour, durability, speed, yield, mana and boss health follow a curve per tier. A new item at tier 3 takes tier-3 stats from the curve, adjusted by its kind's spread (a heavy weapon trades speed for damage). Grades and rolls (spells too) vary inside the tier's band.
3. **A progression graph check.** A test builds the graph of everything and fails if:
   - something can't be reached from the start;
   - a recipe at tier N needs something above tier N;
   - an age's key can't be made by each path's route;
   - a boss's loot or a blueprint sits at the wrong tier.

   It writes a sheet, `PROGRESSION_SHEET.md`, generated like `vitals/SHEET.md`, so a change shows what it moved.
4. **A time budget per age.** The sheet estimates each age's hours from what it costs (amounts × gathering rates × travel). A change that blows an age's budget shows up before anyone plays it.
5. **New content is a branch at an age.** It declares its tier, inputs and outputs, and the check says whether it fits. New dimensions slot in at the tier they need. A new age goes on top. Content packages and servers use the same rules.

World settings can scale costs, boss strength and death's harshness for a faster or slower world,
on top of the same curves.

**The goal** (Ronald, 11-06: "easy to add more content, and it sort of balances it decently well.
Of course, we will need to be able to tweak certain stuff ... if that system's built out good, then
it's already, I think, a huge win"):
- **Declare, then derive.** A new creature, item, perk, spell or boss only declares a few things: tier, size class, role, path, rarity. Its numbers come from the curves.
- **Bounded tweaks.** Hand tweaks are bounded multipliers on the derived values, each with a reason, and the checks still run on the result.
- **Every system, not only combat:** skills, bosses, loot, magic, tames, automation and exploration, all priced in one shared currency of value. Its method is in `reports/Balance model.md` (running).

### The balance model (Ronald's answers, 11-06)

From `reports/Balance model.md`. Everything is priced in one currency, effort minutes. Each
system gets a budget in it. Numbers come from curves, and checks and sheets catch anything out
of band.
- **Fight lengths, as proposed:** for a player geared for the same age, a large dinosaur takes 20-40 s of fighting, a huge one 60-120 s, a titan or boss 2-5 minutes, small ones about 1-5 s.
- **Floors, as proposed:** over-tier gear counts only up to a ceiling against big creatures. A stone-age titan takes at least 2½ minutes even in star-age gear and can still down you in two hits, a huge one about 50 s, a large one 10 s; small ones die in one hit. Damage over the floor goes into stagger and part breaks.
- **Creature levels** (Ronald: "animals should prob have 0-200 acrso the board, so its less of different tiers per biomie, but ofc a dragon should be super strong vs othernanim,asls"):
  - every wild animal rolls a level from 0 to 200 anywhere;
  - the species sets the base strength through its size class and role, so a dragon stays far above a deer;
  - a level-200 animal is about 5× a level-0 one of the same species;
  - this replaces the earlier "a top roll is worth about one age", and the balance model's per-biome area levels apply to places and loot, not to animal levels.
- **Tames in later ages:** carried up by saddles and gear (a higher-age saddle or armour raises the tame), capped like player gear.
- **Damage:** armour / (armour + 5 × hit), capped at 80%, with penetration, damage types, weak points and part breaks.
- **Today's big animals are too soft for these bands.** At the stone age the utahraptor should be about 171 health (it's 60), and a stone sword kills a cedarosaurus in under 40 s. They get re-derived when the curves land.
