# Our changes to Weft's core, and why

Written 2026-11-04 from dev's history. "Core" here means Weft's own crates: `crates/engine`, `sdk`,
`abi`, `render`, `noise`, `app`, the `mod-sdk*` crates and `backends/vulkan`. Our own new crates
(`sandbox`, `packages`, `account`, `bridge`) are additions on top, not core changes, and are
listed at the end only. The crypto side (wallet, DIDs, the chain) is left out of this file.

Since our fork's base with upstream (2026-11-01, 6e4e1685), 214 commits touched the core (of 223 in
`crates/` and `backends/`). PLAN.md rule 4 says to leave the engine alone where we can, so each change
below is here because a feature or a fix needed something only the engine could give.

Each line says **why** we needed it. **Upstream?** marks what might go to Weft one day. Ronald's rule
for now is bug fixes only, written and opened by him.

## 2. Rendering, for our shader pack (Bliss-like look)

Weft's shader packs could only replace shaders. Our pack needed data the engine didn't give.

- **Weather comes in slowly** (Lighting, branch `weather-ease`, 11-06): the client's weather look (`Client::weather_effects`) gathers its sky over 90 s (`GLOOM_SECS`), lets rain fall only once that is more than half there and fades it in over 25 s (`FALLS_SECS`), stops the rain before the sky clears, and asks for rain at nine places 24 blocks apart round the player, so a border blends over about three chunks. Lightning only under a full sky. Packs' rain value (`scene.fog_color.w`) follows the sky's progress, not only what falls, so a pack's own gray sky comes in with it. *Why:* everything went from clear to full rain in five seconds at the spell's start (Ronald, 11-06).
- **Frame look** (Lighting, branch `world-regs`, 11-05): `client::frame_look`. A pack whose sky has `fs_frame_look` draws a row of 8 texels before the world each frame; `Scene::frame_look` gives its index to every shader. chiacraft puts its palette by the hour there. *Why:* the palette was worked out in up to ten places of every pixel, and the Mac's world pass spilled 480 bytes of registers, 77% of it fragment work (Tester's trace, 11-05).
- **Mist volume** (Lighting, branch `froxels`, 11-05): `client::froxels` and `core:froxels` (live, on by default). A pack whose sky has `fs_froxel_light` and `fs_froxel_sum` fills a volume of the view (an eighth of the world's pixels each way, 32 slices, laid side by side in one `Rgba16Float` image, since the backend has no 3D images) after the shadow maps and before the world; `Scene::froxels` gives its index and size to every shader. Its GPU time counts under "shadows". *Why:* the mist walked 8 steps per pixel on every surface (Research rounds 15 and 19, item 2: one of the Mac's dearest parts); the volume walks each cell once and the surfaces read it in two taps.
- **Sun shadows:** a shadow map from the sun for packs (48d38929), from mobs and the player (cc34e2dc), the faces the light falls on (5a096e0f), a coarse far cascade to 400 blocks (f03b29b6), distant terrain casting (c4698cc5), cloud shadows (6b63b917), and a sun path leaned 35° south (5daa6e72). *Why:* real shadows are the core of the look.
- **Depth for screen effects** (3f4035e2), **water that bends and tints what's under it** (df721f33), **light shafts, with fog kept out of caves** (4a875f7e). *Why:* water reflections, contact shadows and shafts need the depth buffer and a copy of the world.
- **Pack options, the "look knobs"** (e2c8496e): a pack names up to 32 settings (16 until the fantasy settings, branch `options-32`, 11-05: `Scene.options` `[[f32; 4]; 8]`) and the engine passes them to its shaders. *Why:* Ronald wanted the look tunable without rebuilding, and every fantasy effect gets its own setting ("we can also have the option to turn on off parts").
- **Held light** (5ae27f4f, 3cdfa80d, b752319f, 3e5ce4b8): a torch in hand lights the way. **The night follows the moon** (fc1478ca), **the night sky** (104c0e39). *Why:* nights are dark by design, so the torch and moon must carry them.
- **Clouds follow the weather and the wind** (5a42062b, 1bae91a3), **biome tints a mod can change while running** (34432291, used for seasons and rain). *Why:* the living-world systems (seasons, rain by place) have to show.
- **Canopy light** (e40f323e, on look-canopy): a biome's air flags carry how closed its canopy is (`weft_abi::air_flags::CANOPY_SHIFT`, bits 16-23, 0-255). The client eases it over 10 s into a new `Scene.canopy` field, the same path the aurora takes. *Why:* the tropical forest's shade and sunflecks under closed crowns.
- **Soft clouds** (Lighting, branch `soft-clouds` from the sync tip, 11-05): `client/soft_clouds.rs`, a setting `core:soft_clouds` (off: the pack's blocky clouds). When the pack's `sky.wgsl` has `fs_cloud_trace` and `fs_cloud_resolve`, the engine traces them into a quarter-size target each frame after the world is copied (`app.rs`, after `copy_behind`), blends that into one of two histories, and the sky's `fs_clouds` draws from it. Three new `Scene` fields: `soft_clouds` (the history's, last frame's and the noise volume's indices, the history's age), `prev_view_proj`, `cloud_moved` (camera move less the clouds' drift, frame number); a 64³ tiling noise volume made once on the CPU (`noise_atlas`); old targets are destroyed only after the frames that used them (a resize must not hang the GPU). *Why:* Ronald, 11-05, "Both, a setting" and "Soft clouds first": Bliss/Photon soft clouds at a sixteenth of the pixels (Research round 15, rec. 7).
- **Screen effects at half size** (Lighting, branch `half-res`, 11-05): a second post hook, `post_low` (`shaders/post_low/<name>.wgsl`, `fn low(p: PostPixel) -> vec4<f32>`): an effect's half-size part, drawn before the effects into its own Rgba16Float image a quarter the pixels, which the effect of the same name reads (`Pass.low`, `PostPixel.low`, `post_low_upsampled`: the four nearest texels weighted by depth). *Why:* Research round 16 #7, #5 and #1b: volumetric light, shafts and the mist march need far fewer pixels than the screen's; the Mac pays 5× the PC per pixel.
- **Sky events** (Lighting, branch `sky-events`, 11-05): `Host::set_sky_event(kind, strength)` and `Host::sky_landing(pos, seconds)` (sdk `Game::set_sky_event`, `Game::sky_landing`, kinds in `weft_abi::sky_event`), a dimension's state sent to its players on a change (`ToClient::SkyEvent`, `ToClient::SkyLanding`), into two `Scene` fields, `sky_event` and `sky_landing`. One value for the whole world, not air-flag bits (those stay for enchanted air by biome). *Why:* Ronald, round 17: blood moons, meteor showers, fireballs and aurora storms, "Gameplay too"; the base mod's schedule (First hours) says when, the pack draws them.
- **Lightning for packs** (Shader, branch `claude/sky-tuning`, 11-05): one `Scene` field, `lightning`: seconds since the last flash where the player is (real time, so tests' pinned shader clock still shows it), a random number of its own, how hard it thunders. The engine's own flash only lifts the daylight for 0.15 s. *Why:* Tester 2's storm shots showed no lightning; Ronald picked "add lightning flashes" (11-05), which the pack draws (bolt, flicker, lit deck) from this. Later (11-05): `lightning.w` holds where the camera faced as the flash began, so the pack aims strikes into view and the bolt stays put while the player turns.
- **Soft clouds on by default** (Shader, branch `claude/sky-tuning`, 11-05): `core:soft_clouds` defaults to 1 (`config.rs`); packs without `fs_cloud_trace` keep their own clouds. *Why:* soft cumulus beat the blocky slabs on Tester 2's and the shader sheets at equal GPU time (t2_perf at 1920x1080: -0.04 to +0.02 ms) (fix 4 sheet).
- **Wet ground for packs** (Shader, branch `claude/sky-wet-ground`, 11-05): the client keeps how wet the ground is where the player is (`ClientState::wet`, up with what falls in ~20 s, down after at a mod's pace) in `Scene::canopy.y`, and `air_flags::DRYING_SHIFT` (bits 24-31) lets a mod's air say how fast it dries (0: about two minutes). *Why:* the wet look followed the rain alone and was gone 25 s after it stopped (Tester 2, 11-05); Ronald picked ground and leaves drying over minutes by the air's warmth and wind.
- **Puddles for packs** (Shader, branch `claude/sky-puddles`, 11-06): the client keeps how far puddles have grown where the player is (`ClientState::puddles`: up over about ten minutes of rain, down after at three times the ground's drying time) in `Scene::canopy.z`. *Why:* the Recrafted study's item 4 (Ronald's yes, 11-06): puddles that grow in long rain and dry in the sun after.
- **Snow line for packs** (Shader, branch `claude/sky-snow-line`, 11-06): `air_flags::SNOWLINE_SHIFT` (bits 2-7, 0 none, else 1 + blocks above the sea in 4-block steps) lets a mod's air say where rain turns to snow up a mountain; the client eases it into `Scene::canopy.w` (blocks above the sea, 400 for none). *Why:* the Recrafted study's item 6 (Ronald's yes, 11-06): rainy peaks get snow.
- **Two-channel entity glow masks** (Lighting, branch `sky-events`, 11-05): an entity's `_e.png` mask goes to the GPU as Rgba8 (`assets::emission_mask_rg`): r its glow as before, g the picture's green × alpha for packs (the blood moon's eyes, Art's masks with red 0). Blocks keep their one-byte masks. *Why:* the mask was one byte (red × alpha), so the eyes painted in green never reached the shader.
- **Half-size effects switch and honest GPU parts** (Lighting, branch `fx-half`, 11-05): `core:post_low` (live, on by default) turns the effects' half-size parts off, so one build compares half against full; and with the world copied for water, the "translucent" GPU part now ends after what is drawn after the copy (sky, clouds, water, particles, the hand), so "effects" is the screen effects alone (Research round 16, M1). *Why:* the Mac's "effects 6.8 ms" mixed in the sky and water, and Ronald wants the Mac's effects cheaper first. Also a fix to Perf 7: under a render scale, its scaling pass took the second effect's entry, so the merged pass read it and the half-size parts' entries as its other effects, and on the Mac only the first effect (reflections) ran as itself.
- **The HDR world, step 1** (Lighting, branch `hdr-world`, 11-05): `core:hdr_world` (read at start, off by default) draws the world into Rgba16Float: `post::world_format()` for the world image, its copy and every world-pass pipeline (world, entities and hand, sky, particles, weather, wires); the screen effects then always run, the last one onto the screen through a pipeline of the screen's format, marked `Pass::spare` 1 so a pack can finish the picture there. *Why:* Ronald's HDR world ("after current looks", then "focus on perf"): the tone curve as one last pass lets the mist and other effects work in linear light at fewer pixels (hdr-world-scope.md).
- **The HDR world's bloom and exposure** (Look, branch `claude/look-reach-bliss-c1cbda`, 11-05): with `core:hdr_world`, when the pack's post.wgsl has `fs_bloom_down`, `fs_bloom_up` and `fs_exposure`, `post::Post` draws a pyramid of the world's picture before the effects (`Pyramid`: Rgba16Float levels each half the one before from half size, then back up), and a 1x1 exposure texel from its narrowest level and last frame's; every effect's `Pass` gets `bloom` and `exposure` (bindless + 1), which the last pass reads before the tone curve. `core:bloom` (live: 0 none, 1 four levels, 2 six, the default); `Scene::frame.y` says the pyramid drew, so a pack's own bloom effect leaves its glow out; `MAX_PASSES` 16 → 40. Also a fix to step 2a: with `merge_effects` the last pass's `spare` was never read, so the HDR picture got no tone curve. *Why:* Ronald, 11-05: "Bliss just looks a lot better than ours still": bloom from real brightness and an exposure that follows the view (Bliss: a 7-mip normalised blur, a log exposure eased over frames).
- **Mist volume, ultra** (Look, branch `claude/look-reach-bliss-c1cbda`, 11-05): `core:froxels` is a number now: 0 off, 1 cells an eighth of the pixels each way (as before), 2 a quarter (`Froxels::cell`, `settings::froxel_cell`), four times the cells. *Why:* Ronald, 11-05: "I dont mind adding extra ultra qulaity settings": a level for strong PC GPUs, not held to the Mac's budget. The volume runs with the HDR world only (`app.rs`: `froxels_wanted` needs `post::hdr()`), so today's LDR look keeps its mist march until Ronald sees the volume there.
- **The far shadow map's reach a setting** (Look, branch `claude/look-reach-bliss-c1cbda`, 11-05): `core:shadow_far` (live: 0 auto, 2000 with the HDR world at no measurable cost and 400 without, as before; or 200-2400 blocks to each side; its depth three times that), `Client::shadow_far_reach`; the pack reads the reach from `scene.shadow_far` (its texel size times its size). *Why:* past 400 blocks every hill and forest was lit flat, where Bliss with Distant Horizons shades the whole view (Planner's gap breakdown, 11-05); an ultra level for strong PC GPUs.
- **The soft clouds' shadow on the land** (Look, branch `claude/look-reach-bliss-c1cbda`, 11-05): `client::cloud_shadow`. With the HDR world and `core:cloud_shadows` (live, on), when the pack's sky has `fs_cloud_shadow`, the sky draws a 512x512 Rgba16Float map before the world, with the soft clouds' own sky entry (their wind and height): how much of the sun gets through the layer at each place, seen from above; `Scene::cloud_shadow` gives its index and size to every shader. *Why:* the soft clouds are a volume the world's shaders can't see, so their shadows came from the blocky clouds' picture at the blocky height and matched nothing in the sky; Bliss's cloud masses throw shadow patches across the land (Planner's gap breakdown, 11-05).
- **Temporal anti-aliasing, and motion vectors for upscalers** (Temporal AA, branch `claude/temporal-aa-f76f4c`, 11-06): `core:aa` (0 off by default, 1 temporal; read at start) and `core:taa_blend` (0.1, live). The render API gets a second color attachment for motion (`Cmd::BeginPass::motion`, `PipelineDesc::motion`, `MOTION_FORMAT` Rgba16Float: xy last frame's place minus this one's in UV, z the writer's depth as a tag, w a reactive share); the Vulkan backend writes it only for fragment entries with a `@location(1)` output (naga reflection), so packs without motion keep working. The world is jittered (Halton 2,3, 8 points: `post::next_jitter`, `Camera::proj`; `proj_plain` without), `Scene::taa`/`taa_prev` carry the jitter, last frame's camera and clock; swaying leaves and grass (the pack's `wind_at`) and entities (`model::PREV_BONES`, last frame's bone rows matched per entity) write their own motion, the hand zero, rain a reactive share; the rest (blocks, distant terrain, sky) is reprojected from depth. `taa.wgsl` (the engine's, a pack may replace it) gathers frames at the world's size right after the world pass: 3x3 YCoCg variance clipping, nearest-depth motion, Catmull-Rom history, Karis weighting, a per-pixel frame count for disocclusion. Its picture replaces the world image for the bloom, the half-size parts and the effects, so the pack's CAS (`base:look_sharpen`) now sharpens the gathered picture instead of raw aliasing. For DLSS/DLAA and Arm ASR: `post::TemporalInputs` (`Post::temporal_inputs`, `export_motion` for the whole motion image `fs_taa_motion`), `set_upscaled` to hand back their picture, `set_jitter_phases`. Builds on the unmerged `taau` branch (Lighting, 11-05; its upscaler is not taken: under a render scale TAA gathers at the world's size and the plain pass scales up). PC check (11-06, HDR, view 32): canopy grain at mc_forest 17.7 -> 10.4, spot C 10.6 -> 6.5, no ghosting; `core:aa` 0 measures as dev, 1 costs +0.07-0.10 ms at 1920x1080 on the RTX 5090 (gallery "Temporal AA"). *Why:* Look found no TAA or MSAA and our leaves stippled and grainy next to Bliss's smooth canopy (`trees_zoom_h12`); DLSS (PC) and Arm ASR (Android) need the same jitter and motion.
- **Performance:** render scale (519dbf32, 14a00a37), a pipeline cache on disk (b1867503), a bounded, depth-only shadow pass (409d70ed), screen effects in one pass (716acfd8, ce011d36), the water's grid only when changed (bdb745ef), small frame-thread steps in place (ac4995f3), the frame thread culling its own draws (c31f6d34), skin painters off the frame thread (1041e730). *Why:* Ronald's Mac lagged at 3456x2234; these took it from about 96-124 fps to 128-154 fps uncapped.
- **Diagnostics:** GPU time per pass in benches (ee147880), slow frames name their step (2394267c), where "submit" goes each second (9ab54ee5, bf07e118), the size the world draws at (ce993538), PACING lines (dcde2415). *Why:* to find the lag and the hitches.
- **Breaking cracks at 32 px** (Art, claude/art-cracks 3fa5964f, merged 0800ffb5): `crates/engine/assets/textures/block/destroy_0-9.png` redrawn 32x32 in place (grown by `notes/blocks/destroy.py`), same names, so `core:block/destroy_N` is unchanged. The pack darkens the face with them (`entity.wgsl` `fs_overlay`). *Why:* the 16 px near-black cracks stamped a chunky black maze on our 32 px blocks (Ronald: "glitchy").
- **Particles the wind carries** (Wind life, branch `claude/wind-life`, 11-06): a particle kind may be `carried` (`abi::ParticleDesc::air`, `sink`, `flutter`, `lift`; flags `FLOATS`, `BLOCK_TINT`; `ParticleBuilder::carried`, `flutter`, `lifts_at`, `floats`, `block_tinted`). `particle_sim.wgsl` gets the wind where the player is, the rain and the shaders' clock (`ParticleSimArgs::wind`, from `Client::particle_wind`) and moves those kinds toward the air's speed there: the same gust bands that bend the pack's plants, eddies, updrafts in a gale's gusts, 15% of it under cover (sky light), less a sink through still air, with a seeded flutter. A fluttering kind is drawn as a card that turns in 3D (`GpuParticle::extra` yzw, read by both `particle.wgsl`s) and lies flat where it lands; once landed, a gust over its `lift` blows it on or up again; a floating kind rides a liquid's surface; a carried one blown out of the GPU's block window ends. A kind may also be drawn as a streak along its motion (`ParticleDesc::streak`, seconds of it; `ParticleKind::motion.w`), for a dust storm's blown sand. Kinds without `air` or `streak` are as before. ## 3. Distant terrain (LOD) and world generation hooks

- **Trees per site, rock on steep faces, stable levels; smooth, morphing far ground; skirts under edges; a tall crown tapers** (747f25bc, 87aa8332, e2c0d171, 0399162b). *Why:* distant forests and mountains looked blocky or wrong, and giant sequoias showed flat-topped.
- **Far trees as crowns over trunks** (branch lod-crowns). Each scatter's shape is sampled 16 times per biome and 8 of the runs kept as crowns (radius, top, lowest leaves, widest layer, how round its top is, trunk width and block, leaves); each far site takes one by its hash and lays it over the cells round or tapering, over the real ground, with a trunk at levels 1-2. A sample comes back as 4 words, not 2, and the LOD compute pass uses 18.5 KB of workgroup memory (was 13.9; Apple GPUs allow 32). The old `TALL_CROWN` taper is gone, the profile covers it. *Why:* Ronald, 11-05: "why the lods still boxes ?": far forests were flat-topped leaf pillars down to the ground, one median tree per scatter.
- **Far steps keep their top's colour down a cell's height** (66a12a45, `lod::mesh`). *Why:* at 3456 px (Ronald's Mac) far terraces showed rows of dirt dashes, one block of grass over dirt per 2- or 4-block step.
- **A dithered fade where the cubes end** (branch lod-fade). The world renderer draws the cube meshes near the view's end with the pack's `fs_opaque_fade`/`fs_cutout_fade`/`fs_translucent_fade` (six more pipelines, made only when the pack has them; the translucent ones since lod-polish, so near water fades too), which dither pixels away over the last 24 blocks; `fs_lod` draws exactly those pixels; meshes past the end aren't drawn (the view's square corners). `Globals.extra.y` carries where the cubes end. *Why:* Ronald chose all three LOD fixes on 11-05; the cube edge showed as a hard line (reports/lod.md:89, Distant Horizons' fade).
- **Far tiles load faster and stand in one level coarser** (branch lod-loading, `lod::pool`, `Lod::request`, `Lod::update`). LOD's own pool has a third of the cores (3 to 6, was 3) at the near-cube pool's low priority (half the cores at normal priority starved the near cubes: 7 s to come in after a teleport, not 1.5); tiles are asked for nearest first in tile widths (coarse before fine at the same reach, the land by the camera before the horizon), each missing tile's parent before it, so a stand-in hides at most its three siblings. Inside `terrain::one_column` (distant terrain's face calls) `Terrain::surface_block` works out only the column asked for unless its cube's are cached (it computed all 256 of the cube column, 1-2 ms, for each far steep cell the dimension's faces asked about: most of a far tile's meshing); elsewhere it is as before (doing that everywhere slowed the near cubes' scattered things, 1.5 to 4-5 s after a teleport). The `load` log times each tile's heights, mesh, face calls and wait, and says how long until every wanted tile was made. *Why:* Research round 16: the far boxes were slow loading (about 20 tiles a second of the 800 a view wants, CPU-bound), and a missing tile's coarse ancestor hid every finished tile under it.
- **The far ground's look setting** (branch far-look, `core:far_look`, `Globals.extra.w`'s top byte; the engine's `world.wgsl` masks it off `lod_mask`'s width). Default 7: the pack thins small plants, softens the cubes' block-scale shading and fades textures to their average colour with distance; 0 turns it off. *Why:* Ronald, 11-06: far ground looked rougher than Voxy's and Distant Horizons'; he picked all three from the sheets.
- **The first far tiles come sooner** (branch lod-first-tiles, `terrain_gpu::lod_desc`, `Lod::request`). Distant terrain's description starts on LOD's threads when the dimension changes, not when the first tile's job is in; the features' heights in flight are capped at eight per LOD thread (three starved a fast flight), so meshes and the description no longer queue behind 256 heights jobs. *Why:* Upstream's perf review (reports/perf-review/far-terrain.md): 3 s of no far tiles after a join or a teleport.
- **The sun-shadow pass has its own globals** (branch lod-gpu, `WorldRenderer::shadow_globals`). `draw_shadow` is recorded after `draw` and wrote the same per-frame `Globals` buffer, so the world pass saw the shadow pass's values: fade end 0, so the dithered fade where the cubes end (`faded()`) was off whenever sun shadows were on. *Why:* found probing distant terrain's GPU cost (11-05): every shader-side probe bit was overwritten the same way.
- **Far crowns' leaves by the season** (branch claude/far-seasons; `terrain_op::FAR_LEAVES`, `FarLeavesArgs`, SDK `TerrainBuilder::far_leaves`, `server::terrain::FarLeaves`, `lod::check_leaves`). A dimension may say what state far crowns of a leaf state are drawn in at a day time; distant terrain asks it while meshing, asks again about every 600 ticks for the states it drew, and makes its tiles again when an answer changed. Base takes the bloom off out of a bloomer's seasons. *Why:* far crowns come from sample trees made once, in flower (worlds start in spring), so far wild cherries stayed pink all year (the dense-forest session, 11-05).
- **Far water at a tile's edge known by its block** (branch claude/far-skirt-water; `lod::mesh`). The rule that leaves water at a far tile's edge without a skirt took a top like the block under it for water, so sand on sand and stone on stone (deserts, bare rock) lost their skirts too where the neighbour's sample stood higher, and the next tile at another level could show a crack. *Why:* found while lighting far slopes (Far view, 11-06).
- **DLSS through NVIDIA's Streamline, opt-in** (branch `claude/windows-dlss5-7ba6fe`, 11-06): the render API gets a temporal upscaler step, `Cmd::Upscale` (colour, depth, motion, output and Ray Reconstruction's guides, with `UpscaleParams` in the list's data: mode, jitter, sizes, reset, the unjittered matrices), `UpscaleMode` (`core:upscaler`: 0 off, 1 DLAA, 2-4 SR Quality/Balanced/Performance, 5-6 RR at screen size and from Quality, 7 DLSS 5) and `Backend::upscaler`/`upscale_render_size`/`create_upscale_target` (Rgba16Float with storage use). The Vulkan backend's `upscale.rs` runs it: DLSS through Streamline 2.14.16 (`streamline.rs`, our own bindings to its MIT headers, behind the off-by-default `streamline` feature, `weft/streamline`), loaded at run time from `streamline/` beside the game (`WEFT_STREAMLINE_DIR`), whose `sl.interposer.dll` then also stands in for Vulkan's loader; without those files the game starts as always. Where no upscaler runs it scales the colour up plainly; `core:upscale_standin` (start) makes that stand-in an upscaler, to check the path on Linux. The engine draws the world at the size the upscaler asks for, exports the whole motion image, draws approximate RR guides (`upscale_guides.wgsl`: normals from depth, the picture as albedo, Karis's specular albedo), and runs the effects at the screen's size on the upscaled picture (`Post::set_upscale`, the client's `FrameCamera`); `post_depth` and the pack's two other depth reads go by the depth image's own size. DLSS 5 (Streamline's `kFeatureDLSS_NR`) has no public plugin or header yet: mode 7 says so and stays off. *Why:* Ronald wants NVIDIA's DLSS (and DLSS 5) as the ultra option on the PC's RTX 5090; Streamline is Windows-only, and the same step serves a Linux NGX path later. Nothing of NVIDIA's is in the repository.
- **A frame number for packs' dithers** (branch claude/fade-per-frame; `Scene.frame.w`, 0 to 63 and round again). base's fade from the cubes into distant terrain moves its noise by it each frame. *Why:* the fade's dither was fixed on the screen, a stipple of standing dots over the mid canopy at view 12 (the Look session, 11-06); moving, the dots blend in time and in the temporal AA's history to come.
- **An imported Minecraft world, test only** (branch claude/mc-world-import; ours in `chiacraft::mc_import` and the `chiacraft-mcimport` crate; core touches: `saves::Storage::save_imported` and `far`, `ToClient::LodFar`, `client::lod` `set_far`/`take_columns`, a hook before `Storage::open` in `app.rs` and `dedicated.rs`). `--import-mc <folder>` writes a Minecraft world's chunks as saved cubes of a new world (they load instead of generating) and Distant Horizons' far data as the dimension's `far.bin`, which distant tiles take over the terrain formula where no saved column is. *Why:* Ronald, 11-06: "let's load that same Minecraft world ... it makes testing even easier": our look against Bliss + DH in the identical scene from the identical camera. Nothing changes without the option.
- **Far faces with an overlay keep their base untinted** (branch claude/far-overlay-tint; `lod::face_of` asks `Ctm::has_overlay`, as the near mesher does). *Why:* far tiles tinted every face's base whole, so a blossoming cherry's pink (its young leaves a tinted overlay) came out brown-red far off in spring: round patches over the forests in the test world's shots (Planner, 11-06). Also acacia's and mesquite's blooms; grass sides only where a far wall shows one.
- **Far tiles tell packs which faces are crowns and how shut in each face is** (branch claude/far-shading; `lod_face::CROWN_SHIFT`, `OCC_SHIFT`/`OCC_MASK`, WGSL `lod_crown`, `lod_occ`; `lod::openness`). Word 0's top bit marks a tree crown's face; bits 29-30 hold 0-3, how much the ground and crowns within three cells rise round the face (the mean over eight ways of the steepest rise); tops merge into runs only at the same value. *Why:* Ronald, 11-05: "in distant hozirons is still 100x better"; the Look session's gap sheet: Bliss + DH's far forests read as clumped crowns with lit tops and dark gaps and its valleys shaded, ours as evenly lit green fields. The pack shades crowns and shut-in faces from these.
- **Far tiles are boxes at every level; far trees' crowns from the top of the tree** (branch claude/far-view-better, 0564285b, 3a3f40f7; `lod::SMOOTH_LEVEL`, `CANOPY_LEVEL`, `terrain_gpu::crown_of`). Smooth tiles are off (past `MAX_LEVEL`; `mesh_smooth` kept); from 8-block cells a crown reaches the ground with no trunk; water and ice cells beside water get no skirt at a tile's edge; a run's crown block is the commonest in its upper half; from 8-block cells a wall shows its top block (bare where the dimension's faces say, steepness by the mean drop: 5d679a65); a sample's crown reaches down to the lowest crown over it (`crown_floor`) and its top is the crowns' mean weighed by cover (`crown_tops`; the LOD pass's workgroup memory 18.5 to 27.7 KB of Apple's 32). *Why:* Ronald, 11-05, after the Distant Horizons + Bliss comparison ("I want to be better"): DH's far land keeps the near land's block steps, ours turned into smooth faceted peaks from about 500 blocks out, curtains hung along far shores, tall trees' logs outnumbered their leaves, and a rainforest kept only its emergents' crowns over bare ground, so far forests stood as bare pillars; far snow ranges came out as stone walls once they were boxes.
- **Scatters up to 96 blocks high** (3cfc8a9c). *Why:* giant sequoias didn't fit the old cap.
- **A continent scale per dimension** (587208ff, f081de3b). *Why:* Ronald picked 1800-block land masses for the overworld (the default is 900).
- **Blocks that stand in still liquid, `holds_liquid`** (c1ee7fe0, cbbeeb9b). *Why:* water plants, kelp and lily pads need water in the same block.

## 8b. Android (branch claude/android-port-3dedde)

- **Android glue in the engine** (Ronald, 11-05: "Can we get the game compiled to Android?" ... "Yes, start it"), all of it idle on a desktop:
  - `render::Backend::surface_lost` / `surface_restored`, and the Vulkan backend's: the surface and swapchain are dropped when Android sends the app to the background and made again for the window's new surface on its return; `begin_frame` draws nothing between. On Android the swapchain asks for the IDENTITY transform (the compositor turns the picture on a phone held sideways) and takes INHERIT alpha where OPAQUE isn't offered (the only one many Android drivers have); a desktop picks as before.
  - `app.rs`: `run_android` (the event loop on the activity crates/android was given; `run` shares `run_on`), `suspended`/`resumed` (pause screen, keys let go, the world saved at once through `ServerHandle::save_now`, no frames and a waiting event loop while in the background: Android may end a background app without a word), the back button as Escape, touch events to `touch.rs`.
  - `touch.rs` (new): on-screen controls over play (move stick, look by dragging, jump, sneak, hit, use, inventory, drop, chat, pause; a hotbar slot by tapping it), pressing the actions' own bindings; in menus the finger is the mouse and a second finger scrolls. On with Android, or `WEFT_TOUCH=1`. Labels `touch.*` in en, ru, fr. `hud::hotbar_slot_at`.
  - `settings::max_gui_scale`: on a touch screen the auto GUI scale fits 400 x 240 GUI pixels instead of 400 x 320 (a phone sideways, 2400 x 1080, gets 4 instead of 2, which a finger can hit).
  - `paths::set_engine_assets` and `set_library_dir`: where the engine's assets and the mods' libraries are when not beside the executable (on Android the executable is the system's `app_process`; mods' libraries come per CPU from the APK's `lib/`, their zips carry only assets).
  - **(Weft bug)** Vulkan: the instance's extension and layer lists are `*const c_char`, not `*const i8`: `c_char` is unsigned on ARM Linux and Android, where the backend didn't compile.
  - `log.rs` + `app.rs` `ANDROID_DESTROYED`: winit 0.30 only logs Android's onDestroy ("TODO: forward onDestroy"), and Android's main thread waits in onDestroy until the game ends, so the game saw nothing and froze ("chiacraft isn't responding", PC emulator 11-05). The logger notices that line and the app saves, leaves and ends. Goes when winit forwards it.
  - **(Weft bug, naga 30)** Vulkan: every `OpSampledImage` made from a `NonUniform` texture gets `NonUniform` too (`non_uniform_sampled_images`, after naga writes the SPIR-V): Vulkan requires it (VUID-RuntimeSpirv-NonUniform-06274), NVIDIA samples right without it, the Pixel 10's PowerVR sampled one texture for a whole group of pixels where two met (black blocks with coloured specks at plants' edges).
  - **(Weft bug, naga 30)** Vulkan: arrays in workgroup memory lose the `ArrayStride` naga gives them (`workgroup_arrays_without_stride`; forbidden there, VUID-StandaloneSpirv-None-10684, three compute shaders): NVIDIA ignored it, the Pixel 10's PowerVR built wrong terrain (ground floating in the air, faces from under the ground showing through).
  - **The water glitch on the Pixel 10**: `Cmd::PassBreak` and `Backend::hand_own_pass` (Imagination's driver; `WEFT_HAND_PASS=0/1`): the first-person hand is drawn in a render pass of its own. Drawn in the world's late pass, the hand (depth writes, `discard` for the body parts it hides) wiped out the blended water drawn before it in whole 32-pixel tiles: squares at the bottom of the screen where the sand under the water showed. Pinned with testkit scripts on the phone: spectator (no hand) clean, survival glitched, `--env WEFT_NO_HAND=1` clean, a pass break around every draw clean. (An earlier guess, no `drawIndirectCount` there, is reverted.) `WEFT_KEEP_COMPRESSION=1` keeps compressed render targets on Android, to compare.
  - Vulkan, Android: render targets are made without the GPU's framebuffer compression (`VK_EXT_image_compression_control`, where the driver has it): on the Pixel 10's PowerVR a target cleared by its pass and then read as a texture (the shader pack's copy of what is behind the water and sky) came back as garbage in every tile only the clear had touched, black and random-coloured squares across the sky. Found with Khronos's validation layer on the phone and the pack's switches one at a time.
  - Vulkan, Android: a "suboptimal" present no longer rebuilds the swapchain unless the size changed: a phone held sideways says so every frame while the compositor turns the picture, and the Pixel rebuilt its swapchain (waiting for the GPU) every few frames. Its frame went from 10-13 to 28 fps.
  - Vulkan: a new texture is cleared to zeros (depth too, which takes TRANSFER_DST for it) instead of left as the memory was; soft clouds clear the history they start from. On the Pixel the soft clouds read leftovers and NaNs.
  - `screens::Loading` and `Running::begin_world`: a world starts after the title picture with "Loading world..." has been drawn, which stays up while the start keeps the frame thread busy (38 s of black on the Pixel: the shader pack's pipelines compiling). Scripted runs start at once, as before.
  - The pipeline cache is also saved when the world has opened and when Android sends the app to the background (Android ends apps without the exit that saved it): the Pixel's second start opened the world in 0.4 s instead of 38 s.
  - `diag.rs`: the process's and the machine's memory from `/proc` on Android as on Linux (the frame report's memory).
  - `world_render.rs`: distant terrain's tiles that lie wholly outside the cubes' mask (`lod_half`, a column's margin) draw with the pack's `fs_lod_open` when it has one (`lod_open_pipe`, kind 6): `fs_lod` without its `discard`, which can't fire there, so the picture is the same. The masked tiles still go first with `fs_lod`. On the Pixel 10's PowerVR a draw that may discard loses hidden-surface removal, and at the vista that discard alone cost a third of the world pass (68.7 ms, 45.7 with none at all).
  - `world_render.rs` + `render`: cutout blocks (leaves) in two passes on Android (`cutout_split`, `WEFT_CUTOUT_PREPASS=0/1`), when the pack has `fs_cutout_depth` and `fs_cutout_shade`: every cutout group's depth first with the alpha test alone (`Blend::NoColor`, new: color write mask 0), then their color where the depth is theirs (`Compare::Equal`, new), with no `discard`. The pack's position is `@invariant` so both passes rasterise the same depth. The same picture; on the Pixel 10's PowerVR a draw that may discard loses hidden-surface removal, and the leaves' discard cost the forest bench 12 of its 37.5 ms world pass (25.5 with leaves drawn solid).
  - `world_render.rs` + `gpu_layout.rs` + `packs.rs` + `client/mod.rs`: a calm world shader. Every shader's prelude has `const CALM: bool = false;` (`gpu_layout::CALM_OFF`). When the pack's `world.wgsl` uses `CALM`, it is composed a second time with it true (`packs::compose_calm`) into a second set of colour pipelines (`calm_pipes`: faces, quads, distant terrain, open tiles, split cutouts, fading cubes; shadows and depth stay plain). The client swaps them in each frame while the scene the shaders see is calm (`set_calm`): no fog in the air (`weather.z` 0), no thunder (`lightning.z` under 0.05), no held light, the eye in no liquid. The pack puts behind `if !CALM` only what draws nothing then (the fog walk, the dust veil, under water, the held light, lightning), so the picture is the same from a smaller shader. On the Pixel 10 the size of those rare paths cost the vista about a fifth of its world pass.
  - `clipboard.rs`: arboard has no Android, so there the clipboard is the game's own; arboard is a dependency only off Android. winit gets `android-native-activity` on Android.

  *Why:* the first Android milestone (title screen, a world, touch movement). Our own parts are `crates/android` (`android_main`: unpacks the game from the APK, stdout/stderr to logcat, launch options from `args.txt`) and `tools/release/android.sh` (the APK without Gradle). *Upstream?* Maybe the surface lost/restored part.
