## 2. Rendering, for our shader pack (Bliss-like look)

Weft's shader packs could only replace shaders. Our pack needed data the engine didn't give.

- **Weather comes in slowly** (Lighting, branch `weather-ease`, 11-06): the client's weather look (`Client::weather_effects`) gathers its sky over 90 s (`GLOOM_SECS`), lets rain fall only once that is more than half there and fades it in over 25 s (`FALLS_SECS`), stops the rain before the sky clears, and asks for rain at nine places 24 blocks apart round the player, so a border blends over about three chunks. Lightning only under a full sky. Packs' rain value (`scene.fog_color.w`) follows the sky's progress, not only what falls, so a pack's own gray sky comes in with it. *Why:* everything went from clear to full rain in five seconds at the spell's start (Ronald, 11-06).
- **Frame look** (Lighting, branch `world-regs`, 11-05): `client::frame_look`. A pack whose sky has `fs_frame_look` draws a row of 8 texels before the world each frame; `Scene::frame_look` gives its index to every shader. chiacraft puts its palette by the hour there. *Why:* the palette was worked out in up to ten places of every pixel, and the Mac's world pass spilled 480 bytes of registers, 77% of it fragment work (Tester's trace, 11-05).
- **Mist volume** (Lighting, branch `froxels`, 11-05): `client::froxels` and `core:froxels` (live, on by default). A pack whose sky has `fs_froxel_light` and `fs_froxel_sum` fills a volume of the view (an eighth of the world's pixels each way, 32 slices, laid side by side in one `Rgba16Float` image, since the backend has no 3D images) after the shadow maps and before the world; `Scene::froxels` gives its index and size to every shader. Its GPU time counts under "shadows". *Why:* the mist walked 8 steps per pixel on every surface (Research rounds 15 and 19, item 2: one of the Mac's dearest parts); the volume walks each cell once and the surfaces read it in two taps.
- **Backed leaf faces** (Look, branch `claude/look-leaf-backed`, 11-06): a face record's bit `face::BACKED` (word 0, bit 15) marks a see-through `CULL_SAME` face whose back face isn't drawn because the same block stands behind it (`client::mesher`, cube faces). The built-in `world.wgsl` (`VOut::backed`, `backed_hole`) and our pack (`kind.w` 2) draw that face's holes as the leaf texture's own average colour in shade instead of discarding them; the shadow pass still lets light through them. *Why:* leaves don't draw the faces between them (Researcher's performance item 9), so a crown's outer shell opened on the sky: from under a canopy 11-20% sky where Minecraft's fancy leaves and Bliss show 0.5% (Bliss's own world, mc_world_forest). The same face count as before. *Upstream?* Maybe, with the face culling it fixes.
- **The bloom pyramid's missing-entry note only for a pack** (Look, branch `claude/look-dim-near`, 11-06): `post.rs` logs `post: no bloom pyramid (...)` only when a pack's post.wgsl is active and lacks `fs_bloom_down` / `fs_bloom_up` / `fs_exposure`. *Why:* the built-in post.wgsl never has them, so every HDR start printed three such lines before the pack came on, and testers took them for bloom and exposure being off on the Mac.
- **Sun shadows:** a shadow map from the sun for packs (48d38929), from mobs and the player (cc34e2dc), the faces the light falls on (5a096e0f), a coarse far cascade to 400 blocks (f03b29b6), distant terrain casting (c4698cc5), cloud shadows (6b63b917), and a sun path leaned 35° south (5daa6e72). *Why:* real shadows are the core of the look.
- **Depth for screen effects** (3f4035e2), **water that bends and tints what's under it** (df721f33), **light shafts, with fog kept out of caves** (4a875f7e). *Why:* water reflections, contact shadows and shafts need the depth buffer and a copy of the world.
- **Pack options, the "look knobs"** (e2c8496e): a pack names up to 32 settings (16 until the fantasy settings, branch `options-32`, 11-05: `Scene.options` `[[f32; 4]; 8]`) and the engine passes them to its shaders. *Why:* Ronald wanted the look tunable without rebuilding, and every fantasy effect gets its own setting ("we can also have the option to turn on off parts").
- **Held light** (5ae27f4f, 3cdfa80d, b752319f, 3e5ce4b8): a torch in hand lights the way. **The night follows the moon** (fc1478ca), **the night sky** (104c0e39). *Why:* nights are dark by design, so the torch and moon must carry them.
- **Clouds follow the weather and the wind** (5a42062b, 1bae91a3), **biome tints a mod can change while running** (34432291, used for seasons and rain). *Why:* the living-world systems (seasons, rain by place) have to show.
- **Canopy light** (e40f323e, on look-canopy): a biome's air flags carry how closed its canopy is (`weft_abi::air_flags::CANOPY_SHIFT`, bits 16-23, 0-255). The client eases it over 10 s into a new `Scene.canopy` field, the same path the aurora takes. *Why:* the tropical forest's shade and sunflecks under closed crowns.
- **Soft clouds** (Lighting, branch `soft-clouds` from the sync tip, 11-05): `client/soft_clouds.rs`, a setting `core:soft_clouds` (off: the pack's blocky clouds). When the pack's `sky.wgsl` has `fs_cloud_trace` and `fs_cloud_resolve`, the engine traces them into a quarter-size target each frame after the world is copied (`app.rs`, after `copy_behind`), blends that into one of two histories, and the sky's `fs_clouds` draws from it. Three new `Scene` fields: `soft_clouds` (the history's, last frame's and the noise volume's indices, the history's age), `prev_view_proj`, `cloud_moved` (camera move less the clouds' drift, frame number); a 64³ tiling noise volume made once on the CPU (`noise_atlas`); old targets are destroyed only after the frames that used them (a resize must not hang the GPU). *Why:* Ronald, 11-05, "Both, a setting" and "Soft clouds first": Bliss/Photon soft clouds at a sixteenth of the pixels (Research round 15, rec. 7).
- **Screen effects at half size** (Lighting, branch `half-res`, 11-05): a second post hook, `post_low` (`shaders/post_low/<name>.wgsl`, `fn low(p: PostPixel) -> vec4<f32>`): an effect's half-size part, drawn before the effects into its own Rgba16Float image a quarter the pixels, which the effect of the same name reads (`Pass.low`, `PostPixel.low`, `post_low_upsampled`: the four nearest texels weighted by depth). *Why:* Research round 16 #7, #5 and #1b: volumetric light, shafts and the mist march need far fewer pixels than the screen's; the Mac pays 5× the PC per pixel.
- **Sky events** (Lighting, branch `sky-events`, 11-05): `Host::set_sky_event(kind, strength)` and `Host::sky_landing(pos, seconds)` (sdk `Game::set_sky_event`, `Game::sky_landing`, kinds in `weft_abi::sky_event`), a dimension's state sent to its players on a change (`ToClient::SkyEvent`, `ToClient::SkyLanding`), into two `Scene` fields, `sky_event` and `sky_landing`. One value for the whole world, not air-flag bits (those stay for enchanted air by biome). *Why:* Ronald, round 17: blood moons, meteor showers, fireballs and aurora storms, "Gameplay too"; the base mod's schedule (First hours) says when, the pack draws them.
- **Lightning for packs** (Shader, branch `claude/sky-tuning`, 11-05): one `Scene` field, `lightning`: seconds since the last flash where the player is (real time, so tests' pinned shader clock still shows it), a random number of its own, how hard it thunders. The engine's own flash only lifts the daylight for 0.15 s. *Why:* Tester 2's storm shots showed no lightning; Ronald picked "add lightning flashes" (11-05), which the pack draws (bolt, flicker, lit deck) from this. Later (11-05): `lightning.w` holds where the camera faced as the flash began, so the pack aims strikes into view and the bolt stays put while the player turns.
- **Soft clouds on by default** (Shader, branch `claude/sky-tuning`, 11-05): `core:soft_clouds` defaults to 1 (`config.rs`); packs without `fs_cloud_trace` keep their own clouds. *Why:* soft cumulus beat the blocky slabs on Tester 2's and the shader sheets at equal GPU time (t2_perf at 1920x1080: -0.04 to +0.02 ms) (fix 4 sheet).
- **Wet ground for packs** (Shader, branch `claude/sky-wet-ground`, 11-05): the client keeps how wet the ground is where the player is (`ClientState::wet`, up with what falls in ~20 s, down after at a mod's pace) in `Scene::canopy.y`, and `air_flags::DRYING_SHIFT` (bits 24-31) lets a mod's air say how fast it dries (0: about two minutes). *Why:* the wet look followed the rain alone and was gone 25 s after it stopped (Tester 2, 11-05); Ronald picked ground and leaves drying over minutes by the air's warmth and wind.

## 3. Distant terrain (LOD) and world generation hooks

- **Trees per site, rock on steep faces, stable levels; smooth, morphing far ground; skirts under edges; a tall crown tapers** (747f25bc, 87aa8332, e2c0d171, 0399162b). *Why:* distant forests and mountains looked blocky or wrong, and giant sequoias showed flat-topped.
- **Far trees as crowns over trunks** (branch lod-crowns). Each scatter's shape is sampled 16 times per biome and 8 of the runs kept as crowns (radius, top, lowest leaves, widest layer, how round its top is, trunk width and block, leaves); each far site takes one by its hash and lays it over the cells round or tapering, over the real ground, with a trunk at levels 1-2. A sample comes back as 4 words, not 2, and the LOD compute pass uses 18.5 KB of workgroup memory (was 13.9; Apple GPUs allow 32). The old `TALL_CROWN` taper is gone, the profile covers it. *Why:* Ronald, 11-05: "why the lods still boxes ?": far forests were flat-topped leaf pillars down to the ground, one median tree per scatter.
- **Far steps keep their top's colour down a cell's height** (66a12a45, `lod::mesh`). *Why:* at 3456 px (Ronald's Mac) far terraces showed rows of dirt dashes, one block of grass over dirt per 2- or 4-block step.
- **A dithered fade where the cubes end** (branch lod-fade). The world renderer draws the cube meshes near the view's end with the pack's `fs_opaque_fade`/`fs_cutout_fade`/`fs_translucent_fade` (six more pipelines, made only when the pack has them; the translucent ones since lod-polish, so near water fades too), which dither pixels away over the last 24 blocks; `fs_lod` draws exactly those pixels; meshes past the end aren't drawn (the view's square corners). `Globals.extra.y` carries where the cubes end. *Why:* Ronald chose all three LOD fixes on 11-05; the cube edge showed as a hard line (reports/lod.md:89, Distant Horizons' fade).
- **Far tiles load faster and stand in one level coarser** (branch lod-loading, `lod::pool`, `Lod::request`, `Lod::update`). LOD's own pool has a third of the cores (3 to 6, was 3) at the near-cube pool's low priority (half the cores at normal priority starved the near cubes: 7 s to come in after a teleport, not 1.5); tiles are asked for nearest first in tile widths (coarse before fine at the same reach, the land by the camera before the horizon), each missing tile's parent before it, so a stand-in hides at most its three siblings. Inside `terrain::one_column` (distant terrain's face calls) `Terrain::surface_block` works out only the column asked for unless its cube's are cached (it computed all 256 of the cube column, 1-2 ms, for each far steep cell the dimension's faces asked about: most of a far tile's meshing); elsewhere it is as before (doing that everywhere slowed the near cubes' scattered things, 1.5 to 4-5 s after a teleport). The `load` log times each tile's heights, mesh, face calls and wait, and says how long until every wanted tile was made. *Why:* Research round 16: the far boxes were slow loading (about 20 tiles a second of the 800 a view wants, CPU-bound), and a missing tile's coarse ancestor hid every finished tile under it.
- **The far ground's look setting** (branch far-look, `core:far_look`, `Globals.extra.w`'s top byte; the engine's `world.wgsl` masks it off `lod_mask`'s width). Default 7: the pack thins small plants, softens the cubes' block-scale shading and fades textures to their average colour with distance; 0 turns it off. *Why:* Ronald, 11-06: far ground looked rougher than Voxy's and Distant Horizons'; he picked all three from the sheets.
- **The first far tiles come sooner** (branch lod-first-tiles, `terrain_gpu::lod_desc`, `Lod::request`). Distant terrain's description starts on LOD's threads when the dimension changes, not when the first tile's job is in; the features' heights in flight are capped at eight per LOD thread (three starved a fast flight), so meshes and the description no longer queue behind 256 heights jobs. *Why:* Upstream's perf review (reports/perf-review/far-terrain.md): 3 s of no far tiles after a join or a teleport.
- **The sun-shadow pass has its own globals** (branch lod-gpu, `WorldRenderer::shadow_globals`). `draw_shadow` is recorded after `draw` and wrote the same per-frame `Globals` buffer, so the world pass saw the shadow pass's values: fade end 0, so the dithered fade where the cubes end (`faded()`) was off whenever sun shadows were on. *Why:* found probing distant terrain's GPU cost (11-05): every shader-side probe bit was overwritten the same way.
- **Far crowns' leaves by the season** (branch claude/far-seasons; `terrain_op::FAR_LEAVES`, `FarLeavesArgs`, SDK `TerrainBuilder::far_leaves`, `server::terrain::FarLeaves`, `lod::check_leaves`). A dimension may say what state far crowns of a leaf state are drawn in at a day time; distant terrain asks it while meshing, asks again about every 600 ticks for the states it drew, and makes its tiles again when an answer changed. Base takes the bloom off out of a bloomer's seasons. *Why:* far crowns come from sample trees made once, in flower (worlds start in spring), so far wild cherries stayed pink all year (the dense-forest session, 11-05).
- **Far water at a tile's edge known by its block** (branch claude/far-skirt-water; `lod::mesh`). The rule that leaves water at a far tile's edge without a skirt took a top like the block under it for water, so sand on sand and stone on stone (deserts, bare rock) lost their skirts too where the neighbour's sample stood higher, and the next tile at another level could show a crack. *Why:* found while lighting far slopes (Far view, 11-06).
- **DLSS through NVIDIA's Streamline, opt-in** (branch `claude/windows-dlss5-7ba6fe`, 11-06): the render API gets a temporal upscaler step, `Cmd::Upscale` (colour, depth, motion, output and Ray Reconstruction's guides, with `UpscaleParams` in the list's data: mode, jitter, sizes, reset, the unjittered matrices), `UpscaleMode` (`core:dlss`: 0 off, 1 DLAA, 2 Quality, 3 Balanced, 4 Performance, 5 Ultra Performance), Ray Reconstruction a flag beside it (`core:dlss_rr`), the model per mode and RR's (`core:dlss_model_<mode>`, `core:dlss_rr_model`: the upscaler's own numbers, `UpscaleModel`), DLSS 5 (`core:dlss5`, `core:dlss5_intensity`), all live; `Backend::upscaler` (`UpscalerInfo`: modes, models, RR, DLSS 5, frame generation, put at start as `core:gpu_dlss*` for base's Video tab) /`upscale_render_size`/`create_upscale_target` (Rgba16Float with storage use). The Vulkan backend's `upscale.rs` runs it: DLSS through Streamline 2.14.16 (`streamline.rs`, our own bindings to its MIT headers, behind the off-by-default `streamline` feature, `weft/streamline`), loaded at run time from `streamline/` beside the game (`WEFT_STREAMLINE_DIR`), whose `sl.interposer.dll` then also stands in for Vulkan's loader; without those files the game starts as always. Where no upscaler runs it scales the colour up plainly; `core:upscale_standin` (start) makes that stand-in an upscaler, to check the path on Linux. The engine draws the world at the size the upscaler asks for, exports the whole motion image, draws approximate RR guides (`upscale_guides.wgsl`: normals from depth, albedo the picture over twice its 5x5 local light, Karis's specular albedo), holds per-frame dithers still and biases texture mips by NVIDIA's log2(render/output) - 1 (`Scene::upscale`, the pack's world shader) while it runs, and runs the effects at the screen's size on the upscaled picture (`Post::set_upscale`, the client's `FrameCamera`); `post_depth` and the pack's two other depth reads go by the depth image's own size. DLSS 5 (Streamline's `kFeatureDLSS_NR`) has no public plugin or header yet: `core:dlss5` says so and stays off. base's Video screen gets a DLSS tab (`mods/base/src/dlss.rs`, en/ru) where the renderer has an upscaler. *Why:* Ronald wants NVIDIA's DLSS (and DLSS 5) as the ultra option on the PC's RTX 5090; Streamline is Windows-only, and the same step serves a Linux NGX path later. Nothing of NVIDIA's is in the repository.

- **Entity send and activation ranges** (249afe81). *Why:* far animals cost the server too much (Research round 2, E1).
- **Entity queries by column while mods run** (Creature AI, branch `claude/creature-ai-3912af`, step 1): `Entities::within` looks only in the 16-block columns a shape (with 2 blocks of slack; the widest box for `TOUCHING`) covers, while `Dim::tick_entities` runs mods (`index_cells`); a teleport (`entity_set` with a position), a dismount and a new entity are added as they happen, a change of the list's order builds it again; under 64 entities, outside that window or for a shape over 1024 columns it scans the list as before, and the results keep list order. *Why:* every query scanned every entity in the region, and mob AI asks once a mob, so a crowd cost the square of its size (`reports/Creature AI.md`, 1.7; the crowd bench, `mods/testkit/scripts/crowd_bench.txt`). **(Weft bug)**: O(N) per query, O(N²) a tick for mob AI.
- **Log lines written off the logging thread** (Creature AI branch): `log.rs` hands each line to a `log` writer thread (the terminal and `latest.log`); `log::flush` (crash reports, and `weft`'s exits through `main.rs`'s `exit`) waits for it, a second at most. *Why:* each line was an unbuffered write to stderr and to the file on the thread that logged; with the PC's disk busy (a build beside the game) the server's mod calls that logged a line waited 0.1-0.4 s on it, a run of one-second hitches in the kills bench (`mods/testkit/scripts/kills_bench.txt`, "slow mod call" lines). **(Weft bug)**: logging could block the server tick on I/O.
- **Mods' time per tick counts only the server's calls, each mod's own** (Creature AI branch): `host::invoke` charges a callback's time to `mod_totals` only on the server's threads (`diag::mark_server_thread`: the server thread and its pool), and less the mod calls made inside it (charged to their own mods). *Why:* the BENCH line's and F3's "mods per tick" added the client's callbacks (an animal's look painted every frame, at 1,000 fps) and nested calls twice, then divided by server ticks: the animals read 13-19 ms a tick inside a 2-6 ms server tick (with no animals at all, 1,897 calls a tick). **(Weft bug)**: per-tick mod time counted client calls and nested time. A server mod call taking 50 ms or more of its own is logged (`slow mod call: <mod> took N ms of its own in a <args> call`), to find hitches.
- **Component reads without a heap buffer** (same branch): `World::component` reads into the value on the stack, and the host copies an entity's bytes straight from where they live instead of cloning them first. *Why:* two allocations a read; the animals read several a mob a tick. **(Weft bug)**: an allocation per read.
- **Items:** a stack's own tool numbers win over its item's (a9fcb688), items that keep when broken (4e03dc83), a stack's look from layers (988c4077). *Why:* tools built from parts (crafting).
- **A player's break drops at their feet** (8cb72890, 6eaf25b5). *Why:* chopped logs landed out of reach (first-day test).
- **A time-of-day rate per dimension, sent to clients** (1e138839, protocol 11 to 12). *Why:* Ronald's slow first day, starting at 10:00.
